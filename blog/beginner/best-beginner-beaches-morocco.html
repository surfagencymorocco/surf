The Bottom Line

If you can only visit one beginner beach in Morocco, make it Imsouane Bay. It earns its 10/10 rating through the combination of a sand bottom, ultra-long rides, an easy paddle-out, and waist-deep water at the lesson area. It is the closest thing to a perfect beginner wave on the planet.

Imsouane Bay — The Gold Standard

Beginner Score: 10/10. Imsouane Bay is, quite simply, the best beginner surf beach in Morocco. The wave is a slow, peeling right-hander that breaks over sand, producing rides of up to 800 metres. The inside section — where lessons take place — is waist-deep, meaning you can stand up at any time. The paddle-out is easy via a gentle channel. There are no rocks, no reef, and no dangerous marine life. The only hazard worth mentioning is the Moroccan sun — wear zinc.

The Bay works year-round, with the most consistent waves in winter (October–April) and smaller, beginner-perfect waves in summer (May–September). Several surf camps and guesthouses sit directly above The Bay, meaning you can walk from your room to the water in under 3 minutes.

Best for: Absolute beginners, nervous first-timers, longboarders, families with children. Not ideal for: Advanced surfers seeking power (head to the Cathedral, the other Imsouane break, or Anchor Point in Taghazout).

Tamraght — Banana Beach

Beginner Score: 8/10. Banana Beach, north of Tamraght village, is a wide sandy beach break with multiple gentle peaks that spread out the crowd. On small-to-medium days (2–4 ft), it produces mellow, rolling waves perfect for beginners working on their pop-up and first green-wave take-offs. The beach is spacious, and the atmosphere is relaxed — you will often see several surf schools running lessons simultaneously.

The main difference from Imsouane Bay is that Banana Beach deepens more quickly — you will be chest-deep within 10–15 metres of the shore. This is not a problem for confident swimmers, but nervous beginners may prefer Imsouane's shallower inside section. An occasional rip current can form at the northern end of the beach on bigger swells, but instructors are well aware of this and keep lessons in the safer central and southern zones.

Best for: Beginners who want a beach break experience, surfers staying in Tamraght camps. Not ideal for: Very nervous first-timers (choose Imsouane Bay instead).

Tamraght — Crocodile Beach

Beginner Score: 9/10. Despite its intimidating name (there are no crocodiles — the name comes from a rock formation that vaguely resembles one), Crocodile Beach is one of the most beginner-friendly beach breaks on the coast. The waves here are typically smaller and gentler than Banana Beach, breaking in knee-to-waist-high rollers that are ideal for first lessons. The sand bottom is consistent, with no rocks or reef in the lesson zone.

Crocodile Beach is the go-to spot for several Tamraght surf camps, and for good reason: it is sheltered, consistent, and forgiving. The beach is less crowded than Banana Beach or Imsouane Bay, even in peak winter. The trade-off is that waves can be very small in summer (ankle-to-knee-high) — but for an absolute beginner, that is not necessarily a disadvantage.

Best for: Absolute beginners, children, those who want a quiet, uncrowded lesson environment. Not ideal for: Anyone wanting more than knee-to-waist-high waves.

Taghazout — Panoramas

Beginner Score: 7/10. Panoramas is the most accessible beginner spot in Taghazout, a 5 to 10-minute walk north of the village centre. On small days (2–3 ft), it delivers fun, forgiving waves over a sand bottom that are suitable for beginners who have already had a few lessons. The beach is beautiful, backed by low cliffs, and you are close to Taghazout's cafés, restaurants, and surf shops for post-surf refuelling.

However, Panoramas is not an ideal spot for a complete first-timer. The wave breaks slightly faster than Imsouane or Crocodile Beach, and the water deepens more quickly. On days over 4 ft, it becomes an intermediate wave and is not suitable for beginners. There are also urchins in the rocky areas at the edges of the beach — stay in the sandy central zone. Taghazout's other beginner-accessible spot is the beach break near Hash Point, but this is even more tide-sensitive and only works on small days.

Best for: Beginners with 3–5 lessons under their belt, surfers staying in Taghazout. Not ideal for: Absolute first-timers (head to Imsouane or Crocodile Beach instead).

Important

Do not confuse Panoramas with Anchor Point, which is a few hundred metres further north. Anchor Point is a reef break for intermediate to advanced surfers only. If you are a beginner in Taghazout, confirm with your instructor exactly which break you are going to — and if anyone suggests Anchor Point for a beginner, find a different instructor.

Essaouira

Beginner Score: 6/10. Essaouira, 2.5 hours north of Taghazout, is a beautiful UNESCO-listed coastal city with a long sandy beach that offers consistent, mellow waves. For beginners, the key advantage is the city itself — Essaouira has excellent restaurants, a vibrant medina, and plenty of non-surfing activities for rest days. It is a great choice if you want to combine learning to surf with cultural exploration.

The catch is the wind. Essaouira is famous as a windsurfing and kitesurfing destination, and the same trade winds that attract wind-powered sports can make surfing choppy and difficult from midday onwards. Beginner surfers must book morning sessions (before 11 AM) when the wind is light and the waves are clean. The beach also has scattered rocks at certain tide levels, so stick to the areas designated by surf schools. Despite these caveats, Essaouira has several good surf camps and is a viable option for beginners, especially in summer when the water is warm and the vibe is lively.

Best for: Beginners who want to combine surfing with city exploration, summer trips. Not ideal for: Those wanting consistent all-day surf conditions, absolute first-timers (choose Imsouane instead).

Which Beach Is Right for You?

  • You have never surfed before and are nervous: Imsouane Bay. The waist-deep water, sand bottom, and gentle waves will put you at ease.
  • You have had 2–3 lessons and want to progress: Imsouane Bay (mid-section) or Crocodile Beach (Tamraght). Both offer slightly bigger waves in a controlled environment.
  • You are staying in Tamraght and want to walk to the beach: Crocodile Beach for your first lessons, Banana Beach once you are comfortable on green waves.
  • You are staying in Taghazout and want convenience: Panoramas (on small days only — confirm with your instructor). Otherwise, camps in Taghazout drive to Imsouane Bay (30 minutes), which is a better learning environment.
  • You want culture + surfing in one trip: Essaouira. Stick to morning sessions and explore the medina in the afternoon.
  • You are travelling with children: Imsouane Bay (shallower, safer, long gentle rides) or Crocodile Beach (small, uncrowded waves).

Beach Safety Checklist for Beginners

  • Always surf at a beach recommended by your instructor — do not explore unknown spots alone
  • Check the conditions before entering the water: wave size, tide, wind direction
  • Identify rip currents — ask your instructor to point them out at each new beach
  • Stay within the designated lesson area — your instructor chose it for a reason
  • Never surf alone as a beginner — always have a buddy or instructor nearby
  • Wear high-SPF zinc sunscreen and reapply between sessions
  • Stay hydrated — bring a water bottle to the beach
  • Know your limits — if conditions look too big, they probably are. Wait for a smaller day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Moroccan beach is best for absolute beginners?
Imsouane Bay is the best beach for absolute beginners. It has a sand bottom (no rocks or reef), slow-peeling waves up to 800 metres long, an easy paddle-out, and waist-deep water at the inside section where lessons take place. Most first-timers stand up within their first hour here.
Are there lifeguards at beginner surf beaches in Morocco?
During summer (June–September), main beaches like Taghazout and Essaouira have lifeguards. However, most beginner surf spots (Imsouane Bay, Tamraght) do not have permanent lifeguards. This is why surf camps and qualified instructors are essential — they provide water safety supervision.
What is the safest beginner beach in Morocco?
Imsouane Bay is the safest beginner beach. The sand bottom means no reef cuts or urchin injuries. The inside section where lessons take place is waist-deep, so you can stand up at any time. There are no dangerous marine animals. The only real hazard is sunburn — wear high-SPF zinc sunscreen.
Can beginners surf at Taghazout?
Yes — but only at specific spots. Panoramas and the beach break near Hash Point offer gentle waves suitable for beginners on small days. Anchor Point and Killer Point are strictly for experienced surfers. Taghazout is better suited to intermediates and advanced surfers; absolute beginners are better off at Imsouane Bay or Tamraght.
Which beginner beach has the best facilities?
Taghazout has the best facilities — numerous cafés, restaurants, surf shops, and accommodation within walking distance. Essaouira also has good facilities and a large medina nearby. Imsouane has basic but adequate facilities (small shops, fish stalls, a few cafés). Tamraght has fewer facilities and requires transport to Taghazout for more options.
Do beginner beaches in Morocco have surf schools nearby?
Yes — all beginner-friendly beaches in Morocco have surf schools and camps within easy reach. Imsouane Bay has camps directly overlooking the wave. Tamraght has numerous camps a 5-minute drive from the beach breaks. Taghazout has the highest concentration of surf schools on the coast.
Is Essaouira good for beginner surfers?
Essaouira can be good for beginners, but with a caveat — the wind. Morning sessions (before 11 AM) are often clean and glassy, perfect for beginners. By midday, the famous trade winds pick up, creating choppy conditions that are better suited to windsurfing and kitesurfing. Beginners should plan morning-only surf sessions here.
What is the water depth at beginner surf beaches in Morocco?
Beginner lessons typically take place in waist-to-chest-deep water. Imsouane Bay's inside section is waist-deep, making it the most comfortable for nervous beginners. Tamraght's beach breaks deepen more quickly — you will be chest-deep within 10–15 metres of shore. Most beginner spots allow you to stand on the sand bottom at any time during a lesson.
Are there rocks or reef at Morocco's beginner beaches?
No — all recommended beginner beaches (Imsouane Bay, Tamraght Banana Beach, Crocodile Beach, Taghazout Panoramas) have sand bottoms with no rocks or reef. This is a key safety feature for beginners. The only exception is Essaouira, which has scattered rocks at certain tide levels — stay within the designated surf school areas.
Which beginner beach is best during winter?
Imsouane Bay is the best winter beginner beach. Its sheltered position and point-break shape mean it handles bigger winter swells better than exposed beach breaks. Tamraght's beach breaks can become too powerful for beginners on big winter days (6 ft+). Taghazout's Panoramas is also a good winter option on small-to-medium days.
How crowded are beginner beaches in Morocco?
It depends on the season. In peak winter (December–February), Imsouane Bay can have 20–40 surfers. Tamraght's beaches have 10–25 surfers on busy days. Summer (June–September) is much quieter at all spots. Dawn patrol (before 8 AM) is the best strategy for avoiding crowds at any beach, any time of year.
Can I walk from my accommodation to beginner beaches?
At Imsouane Bay, many camps and riads are a 2–5 minute walk from the wave. In Taghazout, Panoramas is a 5–10 minute walk from town. In Tamraght, most camps require a 5-minute drive or 15–20 minute walk to the beaches. In Essaouira, the surf beach is a 5–10 minute walk from the medina.
