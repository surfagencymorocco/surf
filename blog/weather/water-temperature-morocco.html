Did You Know?

Despite Morocco being in North Africa, the Atlantic water is significantly cooler than you might expect. At 31 degrees north latitude (similar to Florida or the Canary Islands), the water should be tropical — but the cold Canary Current keeps it refreshingly cool year-round.

What Wetsuit for Each Season

Winter (December–February) — 4/3mm Fullsuit

Winter demands a quality 4/3mm wetsuit. Water temperatures of 16–17°C combined with chilly morning air (sometimes as low as 8°C at dawn) mean you will appreciate every millimetre of neoprene. Add 3mm reef booties — essential not just for warmth but for protection on the rocky entries at Anchor Point and other reef breaks. Many surfers also pack a 2mm neoprene hood for dawn patrol, especially in January. A good winter wetsuit with sealed seams makes the difference between a comfortable two-hour session and shivering after 30 minutes.

Spring (March–May) — Transitioning from 4/3mm to 3/2mm

Spring is the transition season. March still feels like winter in the water (17–18°C), so stick with the 4/3mm. By April (18–19°C), you have a choice: a 3/2mm is comfortable for most people, but if you tend to feel the cold, keep the 4/3mm. May (19–20°C) is firmly 3/2mm territory. Booties remain useful for reef protection throughout spring, though less critical for warmth by May.

Summer (June–September) — 3/2mm Shorty or Boardies

Summer is when Morocco feels properly tropical. Water temperatures climb from 20°C in June to 23°C in August and September. Most surfers switch to a 3/2mm shorty or even boardshorts with a rash vest. The water is wonderfully inviting, and long sessions (3+ hours) are effortless. Sun protection becomes the priority — a long-sleeve rash vest with UPF 50+ is invaluable during the midday sun.

Autumn (October–November) — 3/2mm to 4/3mm

October (21–22°C) is still firmly in 3/2mm territory, and some surfers stay in boardies through early October. By November, the water drops to 19–20°C, and the 4/3mm comes back out. This is the mirror image of spring — comfortable at first, then progressively cooler as winter approaches.

Regional Differences — Taghazout vs Essaouira vs Dakhla

Morocco's Atlantic coastline stretches over 2,500 kilometres, and water temperatures vary significantly from north to south:

Taghazout / Agadir Area (Central Coast)

This is the heart of Moroccan surfing and the benchmark for the temperatures in this guide: 16–23°C year-round. Taghazout benefits from a relatively sheltered position south of the High Atlas mountains, which moderates wind exposure and keeps the water slightly warmer than points further north. This is the most popular area for surf camps, and for good reason — the water temperature range is manageable year-round with the right wetsuit.

Essaouira (North of Taghazout, ~2 hours by car)

Essaouira is consistently 2–3°C cooler than Taghazout (14–21°C range). The city is famously windy — the strong northerly trade winds that make Essaouira a world-class windsurfing and kitesurfing destination also drive intense upwelling (cold water rising from the deep). Water temperatures rarely exceed 20°C even in summer, and winter can dip to 14°C. Surfers in Essaouira typically wear one wetsuit thickness heavier than they would in Taghazout.

Dakhla (Western Sahara, Far South)

Dakhla, located about 1,200 km south of Taghazout, is the warmest surfing region on the Moroccan coast. Water temperatures range from 17–24°C — about 1–2°C warmer than Taghazout year-round. The lower latitude and reduced upwelling effect mean Dakhla is comfortable even in winter. Many kite and surf camps operate here, and the warm water is a major draw for travellers seeking to escape European winters.

Pro Tip from Our Guides

If you get cold easily, book your surf trip for September or October. The water is at its warmest (22–23°C), the summer crowds have left, and the winter swell is just beginning to arrive. It is the most comfortable window of the year for long sessions.

How the Canary Current Affects Temps

The Canary Current is a cold ocean current that flows southward along the northwest African coast. It is part of the North Atlantic Gyre — a massive circular ocean current system — and it brings cooler water from higher latitudes down past Morocco, the Canary Islands, and eventually toward West Africa.

For Moroccan surfers, the Canary Current is a mixed blessing. On one hand, it keeps the water refreshingly cool even during the hottest summer months — you can surf comfortably without overheating, unlike in truly tropical destinations where the water can feel like a bath. On the other hand, it means you will rarely, if ever, surf in boardies alone outside of July–September. The current is strongest in spring and early summer (April–June), which is why the water can feel surprisingly cool in May despite the warm air temperatures.

The current also drives the upwelling that enriches Moroccan waters with nutrients — the same process that supports the rich fisheries along the coast and, indirectly, the fishing communities that became Morocco's original surf pioneers.

Upwelling — Why Spring Can Feel Cold

If you visit Morocco in April or May expecting warm water because the sun is shining, you might be surprised. Upwelling is the culprit. When strong northerly winds push surface water away from the coast, cold water from the deep ocean (200–300 metres down) rises to replace it. This water can be 4–6°C colder than the surface water it displaces.

Upwelling is most pronounced in spring (April–June) along the central Moroccan coast, particularly around Essaouira and north of Agadir. It can cause water temperatures to fluctuate by 2–4°C within a single day, depending on wind strength. A beach that felt warm in the morning can feel noticeably colder by afternoon if the wind picks up and upwelling intensifies. This is something local surfers know well — they pack a warmer wetsuit than the monthly average would suggest, just in case.

When You Can Surf in Boardies

The million-dollar question: when can you surf in just boardshorts in Morocco? The answer is July, August, and the first half of September. During these months, water temperatures reach 22–23°C (72–73°F), which is comfortably warm for most people in boardshorts and a rash vest. You will see plenty of surfers in the lineup with no wetsuit during this window.

However, even in summer, many locals and experienced surfers wear at least a 2mm shorty or wetsuit top. The reasons are practical: sun protection (the Moroccan sun is intense from 10 AM to 4 PM), rash prevention (wax rash on your chest and inner thighs from longer sessions), and warmth during early-morning or late-evening sessions when the air can be cooler. Even in August, a 6 AM dawn patrol can feel chilly in boardies alone.

Local Knowledge

Moroccan surfers have a saying: "If you can count to ten without shivering when you first get in, you are dressed right." The initial shock of entering 17°C water in a 4/3mm wetsuit passes within 30 seconds as the thin layer of water between your skin and the neoprene warms up.

Booties — More Than Just Warmth

Reef booties are one of the most important pieces of equipment for surfing in Morocco, and they serve two purposes: warmth and protection. In winter (November–March), 3mm booties keep your feet warm in 16–18°C water, preventing the numbness that makes it hard to feel your board. Year-round, they protect your feet from sharp rocks, sea urchins, and reef cuts at the point breaks.

Anchor Point, Killer Point, Boilers, and many other Moroccan breaks have rocky entries and shallow reef shelves. At low tide, the reef at Anchor Point is inches below the surface — one misplaced step without booties can result in a deep cut that ends your surf trip. Many experienced surfers wear booties every session, summer or winter, regardless of water temperature. The protection is non-negotiable.

Common Mistake

Many visiting surfers show up without booties, assuming warm water means bare feet are fine. The water temperature is not the issue — it is the reef. Sea urchin spines embedded in your foot will end your trip. Pack booties. Always.

Temperature Timeline

Jan–Feb

Coldest water: 16–17°C. 4/3mm wetsuit, booties, and a hood for dawn patrol. The water feels bracing but the waves are at their biggest and best.

Mar–Apr

Warming but upwelling can surprise. Water climbs from 17 to 19°C. 4/3mm for March, 3/2mm for April. Spring sun makes the air feel warmer than the water.

May–Jun

Temperature accelerates toward summer. Water 19–21°C. 3/2mm fullsuit in May, shorty in June. The mornings are still cool but afternoons are glorious.

Jul–Sep

Warmest water: 21–23°C. Boardies and rash vest for most, shorty for early mornings. This is the most comfortable time to spend hours in the lineup.

Oct–Nov

Still warm but cooling. Water 19–22°C. 3/2mm through October, then 4/3mm in November as winter approaches and the swell machine fires up.

Dec

Back to winter: 17–18°C. 4/3mm wetsuit comes out of storage. Powerful swell returns. The cycle begins again.

Wetsuit Packing Checklist

  • Winter (Nov–Mar): 4/3mm fullsuit + 3mm booties + optional 2mm hood
  • Spring (Apr–May): 3/2mm fullsuit + booties for reef breaks
  • Summer (Jun–Sep): 3/2mm shorty + boardshorts + UPF rash vest
  • Autumn (Oct): 3/2mm fullsuit; (Nov): 4/3mm + booties
  • Reef booties — recommended year-round for point breaks
  • Ear plugs — prevent surfer's ear, especially in colder months
  • High-SPF zinc sunscreen — water-resistant, reef-safe
  • Spare leash — cold water can make leashes more brittle
  • Check your wetsuit seams before travelling — a leaky suit in 16°C water is miserable

Frequently Asked Questions

How cold is the water in Morocco for surfing?
Water temperatures range from 16°C (61°F) in January–February to 23°C (73°F) in August–September. The coldest months require a 4/3mm wetsuit with booties. Summer months are warm enough for a 3/2mm shorty or boardshorts with a rash vest.
Can you surf in Morocco without a wetsuit?
Yes, in July, August, and early September, water temperatures reach 22–23°C (72–73°F), warm enough for boardshorts and a rash vest for most people. However, a light wetsuit top or shorty still provides sun protection and comfort for longer sessions. Outside of summer, a wetsuit is essential.
What wetsuit thickness do I need for Morocco in winter?
A 4/3mm fullsuit is recommended for winter (November–March), with 3mm reef booties for rocky breaks. Many surfers add a 2mm hood for dawn patrol sessions in January and February, when air and water temperatures are at their lowest.
How does water temperature vary along the Moroccan coast?
The Taghazout/Agadir area averages 16–23°C. Essaouira, further north, is typically 2–3°C cooler due to stronger upwelling and wind exposure (14–21°C). Dakhla, in the far south, is warmer — 17–24°C — and is the most comfortable year-round option. The Canary Current cools the entire coast.
What is the Canary Current and how does it affect Morocco?
The Canary Current is a cold ocean current that flows southward along the northwest African coast from the North Atlantic. It brings cooler water from higher latitudes, keeping Moroccan water temperatures moderate year-round. It also drives upwelling — the process that brings nutrient-rich cold water from the deep ocean to the surface, keeping the water cooler than you might expect for Morocco's latitude.
Do I need booties for surfing in Morocco?
Yes, reef booties are strongly recommended year-round for reef breaks like Anchor Point, Killer Point, and Boilers — not just for cold but for protection against sharp rocks and sea urchins. At sandy beach breaks, booties are optional in summer but recommended in winter for warmth.
What wetsuit for Morocco in spring (March–May)?
March: 4/3mm. April: 3/2mm fullsuit or 4/3mm (depends on personal cold tolerance). May: 3/2mm fullsuit or shorty. Water warms from 17°C to 20°C through spring. Booties recommended for reef breaks throughout.
What wetsuit for Morocco in autumn (September–November)?
September: 3/2mm shorty or boardshorts (water 21–22°C). October: 3/2mm fullsuit (water 20–21°C). November: 4/3mm fullsuit (water 19–20°C). Booties recommended from November onward for reef breaks.
How does upwelling affect water temperature in Morocco?
Upwelling occurs when offshore winds push surface water away from the coast, allowing cold, nutrient-rich water from the deep ocean to rise. In Morocco, this is most pronounced in spring and early summer (April–June) when strong northerly trade winds enhance upwelling. It can temporarily drop water temperatures by 2–4°C.
Is Dakhla warmer than Taghazout for surfing?
Yes. Dakhla, located in the Western Sahara about 1,200 km south of Taghazout, is consistently 1–2°C warmer year-round. Winter water temperatures in Dakhla are 17–19°C vs 16–17°C in Taghazout. Summer reaches 22–24°C vs 21–23°C in Taghazout. Dakhla is also less affected by upwelling.
