Shortboard vs Fish vs Hybrid vs Midlength vs Longboard

The right board for your Morocco surf trip depends on three things: your skill level, the season you are visiting, and which spots you plan to surf. Morocco's Atlantic coast offers everything from powerful reef point breaks to mellow beach-break peelers, so the ideal board is the one that matches your ability and the conditions on the ground.

Shortboard (5'10"–6'6"): The workhorse of the Moroccan lineup. If you are a competent intermediate or advanced surfer and you only want to bring one board, a 6'0"–6'6" shortboard is your best bet. It handles Anchor Point at 4–6 ft, thrives at Killer Point, and will still catch waves at the beach breaks on smaller days. Aim for a board with a rounded pin or squash tail, moderate rocker, and enough volume under your chest to paddle comfortably in North Atlantic swell.

Fish (5'6"–6'2"): A fish is a secret weapon for Morocco's summer season (May–September) when the swell drops and the waves get softer. The wider outline, lower rocker, and extra volume mean you can generate speed on faces that a shortboard would bog on. A modern fish (twin keel or quad) works beautifully at the beach breaks around Tamraght, Panoramas, and the inside reform at Banana Point.

Hybrid (6'0"–6'8"): The hybrid board — sometimes called a "daily driver" or "grovel-to-performance" board — is the best single-board solution for Morocco. It has more volume and a wider outline than a pure shortboard but still holds a rail on steeper faces. For a surfer visiting for one week and wanting to surf everything from Anchor Point to Imsouane Bay, a 6'2"–6'6" hybrid is hard to beat. It paddles well, handles overhead surf, and won't leave you frustrated on the smaller days.

Midlength (7'0"–7'10"): A midlength is the board for Imsouane Bay. The long, rolling walls of the Bay are made for trimming and cruising, and a 7'2"–7'6" single-fin or 2+1 midlength will connect sections that disappear under a shortboard. It is also a forgiving board for intermediates who want to surf the point breaks on shoulder-head-high days without the intensity of a shortboard.

Longboard (9'0"+): If you are a dedicated longboarder, bring your log. Imsouane Bay on a clean 3–4 ft day with a 9'6" log is as good as it gets — cross-stepping, nose-riding, and 300-metre rides. The beach breaks around Tamraght and Taghazout also offer longboard-friendly sections, particularly in the summer.

Pro Tip from Our Guides

If you can bring two boards, the dream Morocco quiver is a 6'0"–6'4" shortboard for the point breaks and a 6'4"–6'8" hybrid for everything else. That combination covers 95% of the conditions you will encounter.

Board Recommendations by Skill Level

Beginner (less than 1 year of surfing): Do not buy or bring a board to Morocco. Every surf camp in Taghazout, Tamraght, and Imsouane provides foam boards (7'6"–9'0") included in the package. These boards are stable, safe, and designed for learning. You will progress faster on a camp board with a coach than you would on your own hardboard. The camp will swap your board as you improve — starting on a 9-footer and moving down to an 8-footer once you can consistently catch and ride green waves.

Intermediate (1–3 years, comfortable on green waves up to head-high): This is where bringing your own board starts to make sense, though renting is still a solid option. If you bring one board, make it a hybrid or a fish in the 6'0"–6'8" range — something forgiving with enough volume for easy paddling. Avoid bringing a high-performance shortboard with minimal volume; Moroccan waves demand paddle fitness, and a board that is too small will leave you frustrated and exhausted after two sessions.

Advanced (3+ years, comfortable in overhead surf): Bring your boards. You know what volume and dimensions work for you, and you will want the familiarity of your own equipment on Morocco's powerful point breaks. A two-board quiver is ideal: your everyday shortboard (6'0"–6'4") and a step-up (6'8"–7'2") for the bigger winter days. If you can only bring one, make it a 6'2"–6'6" performance hybrid with enough foam to paddle all day but enough rocker to handle steep drops.

Local Knowledge

The sandbanks at the beach breaks shift dramatically from month to month. A spot that was a hollow barrel in January might be a fat closeout in June. Always ask your camp guide for the latest conditions — they check the banks daily and know which board to grab for each spot.

Should You Bring Your Own or Rent?

This is the most common question we get from surfers planning a Morocco trip. The answer depends on your level, your budget, and how attached you are to your own equipment.

Reasons to bring your own board: You are an advanced surfer with specific board preferences and volume requirements. You are travelling in winter and want a proper step-up. You are doing a longer trip (2+ weeks) where rental costs would exceed airline fees. You compete or surf at a level where the right board genuinely matters for your performance.

Reasons to rent: You are intermediate or below. You are travelling in summer when rental boards handle the conditions perfectly well. You want to travel light and avoid airline sports-equipment fees. You are unsure which board is best for Moroccan waves and want to try different shapes. You are booking a surf camp that includes quality boards in the package — this is by far the most cost-effective option.

How to Pack Your Board for Air Travel

A properly packed board bag dramatically reduces the risk of damage. Here is the method our team uses when flying to and from surf destinations worldwide:

  • Remove fins (or use a fin cover). Fins left in the board are the number-one cause of travel damage. If the bag is compressed or dropped, the fin box can crack from the leverage. Pack fins separately in a dry bag inside the board bag.
  • Use rail savers. Pipe insulation foam (from any hardware store) cut to length and taped along the rails provides cheap, effective protection. Purpose-made neoprene rail covers are even better.
  • Pad the nose and tail. Wrap the nose and tail with bubble wrap, a folded towel, or foam blocks. These are the most vulnerable areas.
  • Pack between boards. If carrying two boards, place a layer of bubble wrap or a folded yoga mat between them to prevent rail dings.
  • Wrap the whole board in a board sock. A board sock inside the travel bag adds an extra layer of protection against scratches and minor impacts.
  • Pack accessories inside. Tuck your wetsuit, towel, leash, wax, and fin key into the nose and tail areas of the bag — this adds padding and saves luggage space. Keep the overall weight under your airline's limit.

Choosing a Surfboard Travel Bag

A quality travel bag is an investment, but it pays for itself in avoided repair bills. Here is what to look for:

  • Padding thickness: Minimum 5 mm, ideally 10 mm. The thicker the foam, the better the protection against baggage-handler drops.
  • Double or coffin bag: A double bag that holds two boards costs little more than a single and gives you flexibility for future trips.
  • Reflective material: A silver or white outer shell reflects heat on the tarmac — dark bags can cook your boards and melt wax.
  • Reinforced nose: Look for a hard-wearing rubber or PVC nose bumper. The nose takes the most abuse in transit.
  • Wheels: Not essential but very helpful if you are also carrying a backpack and navigating airports.
  • Internal straps: Straps inside the bag keep boards from sliding around during transit.

Recommended brands: Dakine World Traveler (excellent padding and durable outer), Creatures of Leisure (lightweight with 10 mm foam), FCS Travel Bag (good value, 5–10 mm options), and Ocean & Earth (budget-friendly with decent protection). Expect to spend â‚¬80–â‚¬200 for a quality bag.

Did You Know?

Some surf camps in Taghazout and Tamraght offer secure board storage. If you are planning to return to Morocco regularly, you can leave your board bag at the camp between trips and only pay one set of airline fees. Ask your camp in advance if this is available.

Pre-Trip Board Preparation Timeline

4 Weeks Before

Check your board for dings and get them repaired. A small ding that takes on water during the trip will ruin a board. Book your airline board-bag slot online.

2 Weeks Before

Remove old wax, check fin boxes for tightness, and inspect the leash string. Pack your board bag to check the weight — add or remove items to stay under the airline limit.

1 Week Before

Pack your board(s) with rail savers, foam padding, and a board sock. Place everything in the travel bag and weigh it. Photograph the bag and board condition for insurance.

Day of Travel

Double-check fin key and leash are in the bag. Attach a bright luggage tag with your contact details to the outside of the board bag.

At Arrival

Inspect your board at the airport before leaving. If damaged, report it immediately at the baggage desk and take photos — airline compensation for sports equipment requires prompt reporting.

Board Packing Checklist

  • Travel board bag (5 mm+ padded, reflective outer)
  • Rail savers or pipe insulation foam for all rails
  • Board sock (one per board)
  • Bubble wrap or foam blocks for nose and tail
  • Fin key + spare fin screws
  • Leash (competition 6' or regular 7')
  • Wax (cool-water formula for winter, warm for summer)
  • Ding repair kit (Solarez or similar UV-cure resin)
  • Luggage tag with name, phone, email (attach to bag exterior)
  • Photo of your board(s) for insurance documentation

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best surfboard for Morocco?
A 6'0"–6'6" shortboard is the best all-rounder for Morocco's point breaks, with a step-up (6'8"–7'2") for bigger winter swells. Intermediates should consider a hybrid or fish (5'8"–6'4") for the beach breaks around Tamraght and Imsouane.
Should I bring my own surfboard to Morocco or rent?
If you are a performance shortboarder with specific board preferences, bring your own. If you are intermediate or beginner, renting is a better option — surf camps in Taghazout and Tamraght offer quality rental boards included in most packages, saving you airline fees and travel hassle.
How much do airlines charge to carry a surfboard?
Most European airlines charge â‚¬30–â‚¬80 each way for a surfboard bag. Ryanair typically charges â‚¬55–â‚¬65, easyJet around â‚¬40–â‚¬55, and Wizz Air approximately â‚¬45–â‚¬60. Always check current fees before booking and add the board bag at the time of booking — it is cheaper than paying at the airport.
Can I bring two surfboards in one bag?
Yes, most airlines treat a board bag as a single sports-equipment item regardless of how many boards are inside, provided it stays within the weight limit (usually 23–32 kg). Packing two boards in a double travel bag with foam padding between them is common practice among surf travellers.
What board should a beginner bring to Morocco?
Beginners should not bring their own board. Surf camps in Morocco provide soft-tops and foam boards ideal for learning. A 7'6"–9'0" foam board is the standard teaching tool, and rental shops in Taghazout, Tamraght, and Imsouane have these in abundance.
Is a fish surfboard good for Morocco?
Yes, a fish (5'8"–6'2") is excellent for Morocco's smaller beach-break days, particularly in summer (May–September) when swells are smaller and less powerful. The extra volume and wider outline help you catch more waves and generate speed on weaker faces.
What size board for Anchor Point?
For Anchor Point, your everyday shortboard (6'0"–6'6") works on most days up to 5 ft. When the swell hits 6 ft+, switch to a step-up (6'8"–7'2") for the extra paddle power and hold on the bigger, faster faces.
Can I buy a surfboard in Morocco?
Yes, there are surf shops in Taghazout and Agadir that sell new and second-hand boards, but the selection is limited compared to Europe and prices are similar or slightly higher. It is not recommended to rely on buying a board upon arrival — bring your own or rent.
What is a hybrid surfboard and is it good for Morocco?
A hybrid board (6'0"–6'8") blends features of a shortboard and a fish or funboard, offering more volume and paddle ease than a pure shortboard while retaining performance. It is an excellent single-board quiver for Morocco, handling everything from small beach breaks to overhead point breaks.
How do I pack a surfboard for air travel?
Use a quality padded travel bag (at least 5 mm padding), remove fins or use a fin cover, wrap rails with pipe insulation or purpose-made rail savers, place foam or bubble wrap between boards if carrying two, pack towels or wetsuits around the nose and tail for extra protection, and add a dry bag with fin keys, wax, and leash inside.
Do surf camps in Morocco provide boards?
Yes, virtually all surf camps in Taghazout, Tamraght, and Imsouane include board rental in their packages. The quality varies — premium camps like SurfAgencyMorocco maintain a quiver of well-known brands (Channel Islands, Firewire, Pyzel) in a range of sizes, while budget camps may have older, more worn boards.
What board is best for Imsouane?
For the long, mellow walls of Imsouane Bay, a midlength (7'0"–7'10") or longboard (9'0"+) is ideal. Shortboarders can still enjoy the Bay on bigger days, but a midlength lets you connect sections and ride for 200+ metres. At the Cathedral point break, a shortboard or step-up works best.
