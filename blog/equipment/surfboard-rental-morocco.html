What Boards Are Available to Rent

Morocco's surf rental market has matured significantly over the past decade. You can now find everything from foam beginner boards to high-performance shortboards and even step-ups in the rental fleets of Taghazout, Tamraght, and Imsouane. However, availability and quality vary enormously between operators.

Shortboards (5'10"–6'6"): Widely available. Premium camps carry recognised brands — Firewire, Pyzel, Channel Islands, JS Industries, Lib Tech. Budget rental shops stock older, more generic brands or locally shaped boards. The shortboard selection is generally best in Taghazout, where demand from intermediate and advanced surfers is highest.

Hybrids and Fish (5'8"–6'8"): Available at most camps and larger rental shops. These are the most popular rental category because they suit a wide range of abilities and conditions. Expect to find Torq, NSP, and locally shaped hybrids in abundance.

Midlengths (7'0"–7'10"): Good availability in Imsouane, where the long walls are best suited to these boards. Less common in Taghazout rental shops — call ahead if you specifically want a midlength.

Longboards (9'0"+): Available at most camps and rental shops that cater to beginners and intermediate cruisers. Imsouane has the best longboard rental selection. Premium logs are rare — most rental longboards are durable epoxy constructions aimed at durability rather than performance.

Step-ups and Guns (6'8"–7'4"+): Limited availability. Premium camps in Taghazout may have a few step-ups in their fleet during winter. If you need a gun for a serious swell, bring your own or confirm availability weeks in advance.

Foam boards and soft-tops (7'0"–9'0"): Abundant everywhere. Every surf camp and rental shop carries a fleet of foam boards for beginners. These are the most consistently well-maintained boards in any rental fleet because they take the least damage.

Quality of Rental Boards — The Reality

Let us be honest: rental board quality in Morocco is inconsistent. The range runs from near-new Firewires at premium camps to waterlogged, heavily repaired boards at budget shops that should have been retired years ago. Here is what to expect at each tier:

Premium camps (â‚¬600–â‚¬750/week): Boards from recognised brands in good condition. Regular rotation — damaged boards are repaired or replaced. The quiver includes a meaningful range of sizes and shapes. Boards are rinsed and stored properly. This is what we provide at SurfAgencyMorocco and what you should look for in any camp you book.

Mid-range camps and shops (â‚¬400–â‚¬600/week or â‚¬12/day): A mix of older brand boards and durable epoxy models (Torq, NSP). Functionally fine but not exciting. Some boards will have repairs and pressure dings. The selection of sizes may be limited — you might not get your ideal dims.

Budget shops (â‚¬8–â‚¬10/day): Old, heavily repaired boards. Waterlogged foam, cracked fin boxes, delaminated decks. These shops exist on price alone. Avoid unless you are a complete beginner who just needs something that floats and do not mind compromised performance.

Red Flags When Choosing a Rental

Spongy or soft areas on the deck (water damage). Multiple large repairs with different-coloured resin. Cracked or wobbly fin boxes. Leash strings that are frayed or worn through. A rental shop that will not let you inspect the board before paying. If you see any of these, walk away.

Should You Rent or Bring Your Own?

The decision comes down to your level, your trip length, and your budget. Here is a clear framework:

Rent if: You are a beginner or early intermediate — the equipment difference will not affect your experience. You are booking a surf camp that includes boards in the package. You are travelling for a week or less — airline board fees plus rental cost roughly even out over 7 days, and you save the hassle. You are visiting in summer when conditions are mellow and board performance matters less. You want to try different board shapes without committing to a purchase.

Bring your own if: You are an advanced surfer who relies on specific volume, rocker, and dimensions. You are travelling in winter when you need a step-up board that may not be available in rental fleets. You are staying 2+ weeks — airline fees become proportionally cheaper than daily rental. You compete or train seriously and the board is an extension of your surfing.

What Surf Camps Include

Moroccan surf camps operate on an all-inclusive model. A typical package includes accommodation (riad, apartment, or surf house), daily breakfast and dinner (often lunch too), 2 surf sessions per day with coaching, daily transport to the best breaks based on conditions, and unlimited use of the camp's board and wetsuit quiver.

The key difference between camps is the quality of the equipment fleet. The best camps reinvest in new boards annually, maintain a size range that covers every guest, and have spares so that a damaged board does not leave anyone sitting on the beach. When comparing camps, ask explicitly: "What brands and sizes are in your rental quiver, and when were they last refreshed?"

How to Check a Rental Board Before Taking It

A 5-minute inspection can save you from a ruined session — or an unfair damage charge. Here is the systematic approach used by our guides:

  • Press test: Run your thumb firmly along the deck from nose to tail. Any area that feels soft or spongy indicates water damage and a compromised board. Reject the board — it will perform poorly and may break under stress.
  • Visual inspection: Hold the board up to the light and look along the rails and deck for cracks, dings, or large pressure marks. Small cosmetic dings are fine; anything that exposes foam is not.
  • Fin boxes: Check each fin box for cracks around the edges. Wiggle the fins — they should be firm with minimal play. A cracked or loose fin box will fail in the water.
  • Leash plug: Tug firmly on the leash plug. It should not move or show cracks around the edges. A leash plug failure means a lost board and a long swim.
  • Document existing damage: Point out any damage to the rental shop staff and take photos on your phone before leaving. This protects you from being charged for pre-existing damage when you return the board.
  • Check the leash: Inspect the leash cord for fraying and the cuff for tears. A leash that snaps mid-session is dangerous in a crowded lineup.
Local Knowledge

Some rental shops in Taghazout operate on a "you break it, you bought it" informal policy. Always clarify the damage policy before handing over any money or leaving your passport as a deposit. A reputable shop will distinguish between normal wear and tear (minor dings, pressure marks) and negligence (snapped board, severe rail damage). Get the policy in writing if possible — even a WhatsApp message is better than a verbal agreement.

Deposits, Damage & Insurance

Independent rental shops typically require a cash deposit (â‚¬100–â‚¬200) or will hold your passport as security. Never leave your passport — offer a cash deposit instead, or use a photocopy plus a driver's license. If a shop insists on keeping your passport, find another rental.

Minor dings (small cracks, pressure dings) are generally considered normal wear and are not charged. Significant damage — snapped board, fin box ripped out, deep rail gashes — may incur a charge of â‚¬30–â‚¬100. This is at the shop's discretion. Always clarify the damage policy before renting and photograph the board's condition on collection.

Travel insurance rarely covers rental equipment damage. Check your policy, but assume you are liable for any significant damage. Some premium surf camps include basic damage coverage in their package fee.

Rental Process — Step by Step

Before Your Trip

If booking a camp, confirm what boards are available and their condition. If renting independently, research shops in your destination town and read recent reviews.

Arrival at the Shop

Ask to see the board selection. Do not accept the first board offered. Explain your ability level and the conditions you expect to surf — a good shop will help you choose.

Inspect Thoroughly

Run the press test, check fin boxes and leash plug, photograph any existing damage. Clarify the swap policy and damage policy before paying.

During Your Rental

Rinse the board with fresh water after each session. Store it in the shade — do not leave it baking in a car or on a hot rooftop. Wax it yourself so you know the grip is right.

Returning the Board

Return the board during shop hours, not at 5 AM when no staff are present. Be present when the shop inspects the board on return. Collect your deposit. If there is a damage dispute, stay calm and refer to your collection photos.

Pre-Rental Inspection Checklist

  • Press-test the entire deck — reject any board with soft spots
  • Inspect both rails for cracks, open dings, or exposed foam
  • Check all fin boxes — no cracks, fins sit firmly
  • Tug firmly on the leash plug — must be secure
  • Inspect the leash for fraying or worn cuffs
  • Photograph the board from all angles (time-stamped if possible)
  • Confirm swap policy — how often can you change boards?
  • Clarify damage policy — what is charged, what is normal wear
  • Get a receipt or confirmation message showing deposit paid and board condition
