Wetsuit Thickness by Season

Morocco's Atlantic coast has a moderate climate with water temperatures that range from a brisk 16°C in February to a balmy 23°C in August. The right wetsuit changes dramatically with the calendar, and wearing the wrong thickness is one of the most common mistakes visiting surfers make — either shivering through a winter dawn patrol or overheating in summer.

Month-by-Month Wetsuit Recommendations

January–February (coldest): This is 4/3 territory, no question. The water bottoms out at 16°C and the early-morning air can be chilly (10–14°C at dawn). A 4/3 mm fullsuit with sealed or taped seams is the best choice. If you run cold, consider a 5/4 mm — though this is rare in Morocco, it is not unreasonable for early-morning winter sessions. Absolutely bring 3 mm reef booties. Hot showers and mint tea after the session are the post-surf standard.

March (transitional): Early March still feels like winter in the water. Stick with your 4/3. By late March, water edges toward 18°C, and a good-quality 3/2 becomes viable, especially for midday sessions. Dawn patrol still favours the 4/3. This is the month when having two wetsuits pays off.

April–May (spring sweet spot): A 3/2 mm fullsuit is perfect. Water climbs to 18–20°C, the sun is warm, and you can comfortably surf for 2–3 hours without getting cold. Some surfers switch to boardshorts by late May, but a 3/2 is safer. Booties remain sensible for the reef breaks.

June–September (summer): Boardshorts, bikinis, and a 1–2 mm neoprene top for sun protection. Water reaches 20–23°C — you can spend hours in the water without any chill. A 2 mm shorty or spring suit is a nice luxury for dawn patrols or if you get cold easily, but most surfers manage fine without. Booties are still smart for Anchor Point and Killer Point due to the reef, but you will see locals going barefoot at the beach breaks.

October–November (autumn build-up): October often still feels summery — water at 20–21°C means a 3/2 is ideal. By mid-November, the Atlantic cools quickly and you will want your 4/3 back. If you are visiting in November, bring both if you can, or default to the 4/3. Booties go back on from November onwards.

Pro Tip from Our Guides

If you are only bringing one wetsuit for a multi-season trip, make it a 4/3 mm with a chest zip. You can always flush water through the neck to cool down on warmer days, but you cannot add insulation to a 3/2 on a cold January dawn patrol.

4/3 vs 3/2 — What Is the Difference?

The numbers refer to neoprene thickness in millimetres. A 4/3 wetsuit has 4 mm panels on the torso (where warmth matters most) and 3 mm panels on the arms and legs (where flexibility is needed). A 3/2 has 3 mm on the torso and 2 mm on the limbs. In practice:

  • 4/3 mm: Designed for 11–17°C water. It provides significant insulation, slightly restricts movement compared to thinner suits, and keeps you comfortable for 2–3 hour sessions in Moroccan winter conditions. If you feel the cold, this is your suit for November through March.
  • 3/2 mm: Designed for 14–20°C water. It is lighter, more flexible, and easier to paddle in. Perfect for April–May and October–early November. Also works for summer surfers who want protection from sun and minor wind chill without overheating.
  • Sealed vs flatlock seams: For winter, look for sealed, taped, or liquid-welded seams — these prevent water flushing through the stitching. Flatlock seams are fine for summer suits but will let cold water seep through in winter conditions.
Did You Know?

Personal cold tolerance varies enormously. A surfer from Norway might happily wear a 3/2 in January, while someone from southern Spain reaches for a 4/3 in April. The recommendations in this guide are based on what the majority of surfers find comfortable — adjust for your own cold tolerance.

Booties: When You Need Them

Booties in Morocco are less about cold and more about protection. The point breaks — Anchor Point, Killer Point, Dracula, The Cathedral — break over sharp rock and reef. At low tide, the reef is exposed, and walking in or out without booties is asking for a cut foot, a sea-urchin spine, or both.

For the beach breaks (Panoramas, Devil's Rock, Imsouane Bay sand-bottom sections), booties are optional. In winter, they add welcome warmth when the sand is cold underfoot at dawn. In summer, you can comfortably go barefoot at the sand-bottom breaks.

What to look for in reef booties: A 3 mm split-toe boot with a durable sole (not a smoothskin dive boot — those disintegrate on reef). Brands like O'Neill, Rip Curl, and Billabong make purpose-built reef booties with reinforced toes and heels. Expect to pay â‚¬25–â‚¬45. Bring a spare pair — booties take a beating on Moroccan reef and a torn pair halfway through your trip is frustrating.

Do Not Skip Booties at Anchor Point

Anchor Point at low tide exposes a reef shelf that is covered in sharp rock and sea urchins. We have seen too many surfers end their trip early with deep foot cuts from going barefoot on the rocks. Booties are the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.

Hood & Gloves — Do You Need Them?

Short answer: no. Morocco's water never drops to temperatures that require a hood or gloves for thermal protection. The coldest the water gets is 16°C in February, which is well within the comfort range of a 4/3 mm wetsuit without additional neoprene accessories. Air temperatures in winter hover between 15–22°C in the daytime, so the wind chill on your face when sitting in the lineup is minimal.

The only exception: if you are surfing a January dawn patrol at Anchor Point on a windy morning, a thin neoprene cap might add a touch of comfort. But this is a personal preference — Moroccan locals never wear hoods or gloves, and most visiting European surfers find them unnecessary. Save the luggage space for an extra leash or a second set of booties instead.

How to Pack & Dry Your Wetsuit

Packing for Travel

  • Ensure the wetsuit is 100% dry before packing. A damp wetsuit in a sealed bag for 24 hours develops mildew that is nearly impossible to remove.
  • Fold the wetsuit loosely at the waist — avoid sharp creases at the knees and shoulders, which can weaken the neoprene over time.
  • Place it in a mesh bag (so it can breathe during transit) or a dry bag. Pack it in your checked luggage or inside your board bag as extra padding around the nose and tail of your board.

Drying Between Sessions

  • Rinse with fresh water after every session. Most surf camps have outdoor showers or hoses for this purpose.
  • Hang on a wide-shouldered hanger (never a wire hanger or a single peg — this stretches the shoulders).
  • Hang inside-out first. Once the inside is dry (usually 2–4 hours in the Moroccan sun), turn it right-side out to dry the exterior.
  • Always dry in the shade. Direct summer sun degrades neoprene surprisingly fast — a few hours in strong Moroccan sun can noticeably age a wetsuit.
  • Rooftop terraces are standard wetsuit-drying zones at Moroccan riads and surf camps — they catch the breeze perfectly.
Local Knowledge

Most surf camps in Taghazout and Tamraght have designated wetsuit-drying areas on their rooftops with wide hangers and fresh-water hoses. If you stay at a riad, ask where the drying area is on arrival — you do not want to be the guest draping a dripping wetsuit over a balcony railing.

Session Timeline — What to Wear & When

06:30 — Dawn Patrol (Winter)

4/3 mm fullsuit, 3 mm booties. Air is 10–14°C, water 16°C. The hardest moment is taking off your warm clothes in the car park. Once you are in the water, you will be fine.

10:00 — Morning Session (Spring)

3/2 mm fullsuit. Sun is up, air 18–22°C, water 17–19°C. Perfect conditions for a long session without overheating or getting cold.

13:00 — Midday Session (Summer)

Boardies or bikini, optional 1 mm neoprene top for sun protection. Water 22°C. You can surf for hours. Reapply zinc sunscreen every 90 minutes.

16:00 — Afternoon Session (Autumn)

3/2 mm (October) or 4/3 mm (November). Water is 18–21°C but cooling. The afternoon offshore wind can add a chill — a wind-resistant outer layer helps.

Sunset — Post-Surf

Rinse wetsuit with fresh water, hang inside-out on a wide hanger in the shade. Hot shower, change into dry clothes, rooftop mint tea. Tomorrow you do it all over again.

Wetsuit Packing Checklist

  • 4/3 mm fullsuit (sealed seams) — winter trips Nov–Mar
  • 3/2 mm fullsuit — shoulder season (Oct, Apr–May) or as a second suit
  • 2 mm shorty or neoprene top — summer trips or sun protection
  • 3 mm reef booties (split-toe, durable sole) — year-round for reef breaks
  • Spare booties (optional but recommended for winter trips)
  • Wide-shouldered plastic hanger for drying between sessions
  • Mesh bag or dry bag for transporting a damp wetsuit on departure day
  • Ear plugs (SurfEars or similar) — prevents surfer's ear in cold water
  • Wetsuit shampoo or mild detergent for a deep clean on return home

Frequently Asked Questions

What wetsuit thickness do I need for Morocco?
A 4/3 mm fullsuit for winter (November–March), a 3/2 mm fullsuit for shoulder seasons (October, April–May), and boardshorts or a 2 mm shorty for summer (June–September). If you are bringing only one wetsuit, make it a 4/3 mm — you can always let water in to cool down, but you cannot add warmth.
Do I need booties for surfing in Morocco?
Yes, reef booties are strongly recommended year-round for the point breaks (Anchor Point, Killer Point, Dracula) due to sharp rock, reef, and sea urchins. For pure beach breaks (Panoramas, Devil's Rock, Imsouane Bay sand sections), booties are optional but still useful in winter when water drops to 16°C.
Can I rent a wetsuit in Morocco?
Yes, nearly all surf camps and rental shops in Taghazout, Tamraght, and Imsouane offer wetsuit rentals, usually included in the camp package. Rental wetsuits are typically 4/3 mm in winter and 3/2 mm in summer. Quality varies by camp — premium camps maintain newer, well-rinsed wetsuits.
Do I need a hood or gloves in Morocco?
Rarely. Morocco's water never drops below 16°C, and air temperatures in winter are still mild (15–22°C during the day). A hood is unnecessary for 99% of sessions. Gloves might be appreciated on the coldest January dawn patrols, but most local surfers never use them.
What is the water temperature in Morocco?
Winter (Dec–Feb): 16–18°C. Spring (Mar–May): 17–20°C. Summer (Jun–Sep): 20–23°C. Autumn (Oct–Nov): 18–21°C. These are multi-year Atlantic Morocco coastal averages — actual temperatures vary by upwellings and offshore winds.
Can I surf in boardshorts in Morocco?
Yes, from June through September, boardshorts or a bikini are comfortable for most surfers. A 1–2 mm neoprene top provides sun protection and warmth for early-morning sessions. Outside summer, a wetsuit is needed.
How do I pack a wetsuit for travel?
Make sure the wetsuit is completely dry before packing. Fold it loosely (avoid sharp creases on the neoprene), place it in a mesh bag or dry bag, and pack it in your checked luggage or board bag. Never store a wet or damp wetsuit in a sealed bag — mildew grows quickly.
How should I dry my wetsuit between sessions in Morocco?
Most surf camps and riads have rooftop terraces perfect for drying wetsuits. Hang your wetsuit inside-out on a wide hanger in the shade — direct Moroccan sun degrades neoprene quickly. Turn it right-side out once the inside is dry. A second wetsuit is a luxury that solves the damp-suit problem entirely.
Is a 3/2 wetsuit enough for Morocco in March?
March is transitional. Early March (water ~17°C) can be chilly, and a 4/3 mm is more comfortable for dawn patrols. By late March and April, a 3/2 mm is sufficient for most surfers, especially if you run warm. If you only have a 3/2, bring booties and you will manage.
What is the difference between a 4/3 and a 3/2 wetsuit?
A 4/3 wetsuit has 4 mm neoprene on the torso and 3 mm on the arms and legs, designed for water temperatures of roughly 11–17°C. A 3/2 has 3 mm on the torso and 2 mm on the limbs, suitable for 14–20°C. In Morocco, the 4/3 is the winter standard; the 3/2 covers spring and autumn.
Should I bring two wetsuits to Morocco?
For a winter trip, two wetsuits (ideally two 4/3s or a 4/3 plus a 3/2) let you rotate and always have a dry suit for the afternoon session. This is a luxury, not a necessity. For summer, a single 3/2 or boardshorts plus a neoprene top is all you need.
Do surf camps wash wetsuits in Morocco?
Quality camps rinse rental wetsuits in fresh water daily and hang them to dry. Budget camps may not — ask when booking. If you are renting, bring your own wetsuit if hygiene is a concern. Our camp uses fresh-water rinsing and weekly mild-detergent cleaning for all rental wetsuits.
