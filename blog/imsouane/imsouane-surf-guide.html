What Is Imsouane?

Imsouane is a small fishing village on Morocco's Atlantic coast, roughly 90 kilometres north of Agadir. Once a well-kept secret among travelling surfers, it has quietly become one of the most celebrated surf destinations in Africa — not for its power or barrel sections, but for something rarer: the longest rideable wave on the continent.

The village sits in a wide, sandy bay framed by low cliffs. On the southern side, a long right-hand point break — known simply as The Bay — peels for up to 800 metres over a forgiving sand and rock bottom. This is Africa's longest wave, and it is one of the very few places on Earth where a beginner can ride a wave for over a minute on their first lesson.

But Imsouane is not just for beginners. Just north of The Bay, around a rocky headland, lies the Cathedral — a faster, punchier wave that breaks over a combination of rock shelf and sand. On a solid swell, the Cathedral offers steep drops, long walls, and enough speed to keep even advanced surfers satisfied. Together, these two breaks make Imsouane one of the most versatile surf destinations anywhere: beginners and longboarders dominate The Bay, while intermediates and advanced surfers work the Cathedral.

Did You Know?

The name "Imsouane" comes from the Amazigh (Berber) word for "mouth" — a reference to the river mouth at the southern end of the bay. For centuries, this was simply a small fishing harbour. Surfers discovered it in the 1970s, but it was not until the 2000s that the village began attracting international attention for its legendary long rides.

The Bay — Africa's Longest Wave

The Bay at Imsouane is the village's crown jewel and the reason most people make the journey here. It is a right-hand point break that peels along the southern side of the bay, working best on a northwest or west-northwest swell. What makes The Bay truly special is its length: on a clean, head-high day, a surfer can ride a single wave for 600 to 800 metres — well over a minute of continuous surfing.

The wave breaks in three distinct sections. The outside take-off is the steepest part, where the swell first hits the point and jacks up into a smooth, open face. This is where you will see longboarders cross-stepping and noseriding with casual elegance. The mid-section is the longest and most consistent: a rolling, thigh-to-waist-high wall that breaks slowly enough for beginners to practise pop-ups and turns without feeling rushed. The inside section — closest to the beach — often reforms into a soft, forgiving shorebreak perfect for practising your final turns before kicking out onto the sand.

Why The Bay Is Perfect for Beginners

If you have never surfed before, The Bay at Imsouane is about as ideal as a wave can get. Here is why:

  • Sand bottom. The take-off and most of the ride are over sand — no reef, no rocks, no urchins. Falling off is safe and painless.
  • Slow, forgiving peel. The wave breaks softly and predictably, giving beginners plenty of time to get to their feet and settle into their stance.
  • Long, white-water rides. Even if you stay in the white water, the reform provides hundreds of metres of rideable foam.
  • Easy paddle-out. The channel on the inside makes paddling back out relatively effortless, meaning more time riding waves and less time fighting currents.
  • Warm water (for most of the year). You spend less energy staying warm and more energy learning.

The Bay as a Longboarder's Paradise

Longboarders travel to Imsouane from every corner of the globe for good reason. The Bay's slow, groomed face is tailor-made for classic longboarding: noserides, cross-steps, drop-knee turns, and hang-fives all come naturally here. On a waist-high day with a clean swell, you can noseride for 10 to 15 seconds on a single section — something virtually impossible on most beach breaks. If you ride a log, Imsouane belongs on your bucket list.

Pro Tip / Astuce SurfAgencyMorocco

Want the longest ride of your life? Position yourself at the far outside peak on a rising mid-tide with a clean NW swell. Take off early, hold a high line, and do not pump — let the wave carry you. You can ride from the point almost all the way to the river mouth. Our guides know the exact take-off spot for maximum distance.

The Cathedral — Imsouane's Faster Break

Walk north past the fishing harbour and around the rocky headland, and you will arrive at the Cathedral. Named for the towering cliff that overlooks the break — which local surfers say resembles a cathedral spire — this is Imsouane's punchier, more powerful wave.

The Cathedral is a right-hand point break that works best on a NW to WNW swell. Unlike The Bay's sandy, forgiving face, the Cathedral breaks over a rock shelf at the take-off before transitioning to sand further inside. On a 3–4 ft swell, it offers a steep, clean drop followed by a fast, workable wall with sections for turns, cutbacks, and the occasional cover-up. On bigger days (5–6 ft+), the Cathedral becomes a serious wave — fast, hollow, and not for the faint-hearted.

Important — Cathedral Is Not for Beginners

Despite being only a 10-minute walk from The Bay, the Cathedral is a very different wave. The rock shelf, faster speed, and more powerful sections make it unsuitable for anyone who cannot confidently handle a green wave and paddle through a lineup. If you are unsure of your level, stick to The Bay or ask your surf guide for an honest assessment.

Who Is Imsouane For?

Imsouane is one of the rare surf destinations that genuinely works for everyone — but different groups get different things from it. Here is our honest breakdown:

  • Absolute beginners: The Bay is arguably the best learner wave in Morocco, and possibly the world. Long, slow, forgiving rides on a sand bottom with an easy paddle-out. Most first-timers stand up within their first hour and ride a green wave by day two or three.
  • Intermediate surfers: You have two excellent options. On smaller days, The Bay lets you work on turns, cross-steps, and wave-reading without pressure. On bigger days, the Cathedral pushes your limits with steeper drops and faster walls — ideal for progressing from intermediate to advanced.
  • Advanced surfers: The Cathedral on a solid swell (4–6 ft+) delivers powerful, high-performance waves. The take-off is critical, the wall is fast, and the inside section can barrel. Pair it with dawn patrols to Taghazout's Anchor Point (30 minutes south) on the biggest days, and you have a world-class surf trip.
  • Longboarders: This is your paradise. Bring a 9'+ log and prepare for the longest noserides of your life. The Bay at Imsouane is regularly cited in top-10 lists of the world's best longboard waves.
  • Families & non-surfing partners: Imsouane's mellow village atmosphere, fresh seafood, and beautiful beach make it a great destination even for those who do not surf. Watching the sunset over The Bay with a mint tea in hand is a highlight for anyone.

TideAll tides (best on mid-rising)Mid to high (avoid dead low)

The Bay is remarkably consistent and works on most tides. The ideal scenario is a rising mid-tide in the morning with a clean NW groundswell and light offshore wind from the east. Under these conditions, the wave peels with mechanical perfection from the point all the way to the inside sandbank.

The Cathedral is more tide-sensitive. At dead low tide, the rock shelf is dangerously exposed — avoid paddling out at this time. Mid to high tide produces the best shape, with enough water over the rocks to create a smooth face. An incoming tide in the morning, combined with a NW swell and offshore wind, is the dream combination.

Pro Tip from Our Guides

If the swell is too big for The Bay (6 ft+), the wave can close out and become difficult. On these days, head to the Cathedral instead — or drive 30 minutes south to Taghazout's Killer Point, which handles bigger swells beautifully. Our guides make the call every morning based on live conditions.

Local Knowledge

The Bay holds its shape best between October and March. In the summer months, the sandbank shifts southwards towards the river mouth, and the wave becomes shorter but punchier — still fun, but different. Ask a local guide where the sandbank is sitting before paddling out.

Best Seasons to Surf Imsouane

  • October–November: The season wakes up. Consistent waist-to-head-high swells, warm air (20–25°C), water still comfortable at 19–21°C. Crowds are manageable. An excellent window for all levels.
  • December–February: Peak season. The most consistent swells of the year — head-high to overhead days are common. Water drops to 16–18°C, so bring a 4/3mm wetsuit. The Bay can get crowded, but the long wave handles crowd pressure better than most breaks. Cathedral fires on the bigger swells.
  • March–April: A sweet spot. Still consistent swell but slightly smaller on average. Water warms up (17–19°C), the weather is beautiful, and crowds thin out as the European surf-trip season winds down. Many local guides say March is the single best month to visit Imsouane.
  • May–September: Summer. Swells are smaller and less frequent, but knee-to-waist-high days still come through regularly. Water warms to 20–23°C — you can surf in boardshorts or a 2mm top. The Bay is quiet, the village is peaceful, and it is the best window for a relaxed, uncrowded longboard trip. Not ideal if you are hunting big waves.

The Village — What to Expect

Imsouane is not a resort town. It is a working fishing village first, a surf destination second. In the early morning, you will see wooden fishing boats being hauled up the beach and fish being auctioned directly from the sand at the harbour. The smell of grilled sardines and fresh msemen (Moroccan pancakes) fills the air from roadside stalls.

The village is small enough to walk across in 15 minutes. There are a handful of small surf shops, several cafés with terraces overlooking The Bay, and a growing number of surf camps and guesthouses. The vibe is overwhelmingly relaxed — nobody is in a hurry in Imsouane, and that is precisely the point.

Key landmarks:

  • The harbour: The heart of the village. In the morning, it is alive with fishermen, fishmongers, and the occasional cat trying to negotiate a free breakfast. In the evening, it is the best place to watch the sunset with a mint tea.
  • The cliff (Cathedral viewpoint): A short walk north of the harbour. Offers a panoramic view of both The Bay and the Cathedral. The perfect spot for checking conditions before you paddle out, or just soaking in the Atlantic panorama.
  • The river mouth: The southern end of The Bay, where a small river meets the ocean. The wave often reforms here, creating a fun inside section.
  • Surf cafés: A growing scene of casual, surfer-owned cafés line the road above The Bay. Most serve excellent coffee, smoothies, and Moroccan staples like tagine and couscous.

How to Get to Imsouane

Imsouane is approximately 90 km north of Agadir Al-Massira Airport (AGA), which is served by direct flights from major European cities including Paris, London, Warsaw, Berlin, Amsterdam, Barcelona, and Brussels (as of 2026 — routes change seasonally).

From Agadir airport, the drive takes about 1 hour and 30 minutes. The road is well-maintained, and the final stretch offers stunning coastal views. Most surf camps in Imsouane include airport transfers in their packages, which is the easiest option. Independent travellers can rent a car at the airport (recommended for flexibility) or take a grand taxi from Agadir.

If you are driving yourself, follow the N1 coastal road north from Agadir, then take the P1007 towards Imsouane. The last 20 km on the P1007 is a winding mountain road — take it slowly, especially at night. The drive is beautiful but requires attention.

The Lineup — Local Etiquette

Imsouane's lineup is generally more relaxed than Taghazout's, but the same principles of respect apply:

  • Respect the locals. Many of Imsouane's surfers grew up here. Greet people, smile, wait your turn. A good attitude earns respect in return.
  • Share the long rides. Because The Bay produces such long waves, it is common — and encouraged — to share waves. If you are on the inside and someone who paddled back out is positioned to pick up the same wave further down the line, let them have that section.
  • Do not drop in. This is universal surf etiquette. At The Bay, dropping in is particularly egregious because the wave is so long — the person you dropped in on could have been riding for another 200 metres.
  • Watch for longboarders. Longboards cover distance fast. If you are on a shortboard, be aware that longboarders can catch the wave much earlier and will be riding it from far outside.

Hazards & Safety

  • Cathedral reef. The take-off zone at the Cathedral is over a rock shelf. At low tide, it is dangerously shallow. Booties are recommended here.
  • Rip currents. On bigger swells (5 ft+), a strong rip can run along the northern side of The Bay, near the harbour wall. If caught, paddle across the rip (parallel to the shore), not against it.
  • Sea urchins. Present in the rocky areas near the Cathedral and the harbour. Wear reef booties when walking on rocks.
  • Fishing boats. The Bay is also a working harbour. Small wooden fishing boats launch from the harbour and occasionally cross the lineup. Keep an eye out and give them space — they were here first.
  • Sun exposure. Morocco's sun is strong, even in winter. High-SPF zinc sunscreen and a hat for when you are watching from the cliff are essentials.

If you are an experienced surfer travelling with your own quiver, bring a longboard for The Bay and a shortboard or step-up for the Cathedral. If you can only bring one board, a 7'+ funboard or mid-length is the best compromise — it will work at both breaks.

06:30

Wake up. Walk to the cliff with a coffee. Watch the sunrise and check conditions — The Bay and Cathedral both visible from the viewpoint.

07:00

Dawn patrol at The Bay. Glassy conditions, empty lineup, the longest rides of the day.

09:00

Breakfast at a surf café overlooking The Bay. Msemen pancakes, fresh orange juice, mint tea.

10:30

Second session. Beginners take a lesson in the white water at the inside section. Intermediates and advanced surfers check the Cathedral as the tide fills in.

13:00

Lunch at the harbour. Fresh grilled fish, Moroccan salad, bread, and olives. Followed by a siesta or a walk through the village.

16:00

Afternoon surf session — often the wind stays light, and the late afternoon light is magical on the water.

Sunset

Watch the sunset from the cliff with a mint tea. Dinner at a local restaurant — tagine, couscous, or more fresh fish.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes Imsouane special for surfing?
Imsouane is home to The Bay, Africa's longest wave — a gentle right-hand point break that can produce rides of up to 800 metres. It is widely considered one of the best beginner and longboard waves in the world, thanks to its slow, forgiving peel and sandy bottom.
Is Imsouane good for beginners?
Yes — The Bay at Imsouane is arguably the single best beginner wave in Morocco, and one of the best in the world. The wave peels slowly over a sand bottom with no reef, no rocks, and very few hazards. First-timers routinely ride 50 to 100 metres on their very first lesson.
How long is the ride at The Bay Imsouane?
On a good day, the ride at The Bay can be 600 to 800 metres — easily over a minute on the wave. On smaller days, rides of 200 to 400 metres are typical. This is what makes it Africa's longest rideable wave.
When is the best time to surf Imsouane?
October through April is the main season, with December to February producing the most consistent swells. The summer months (May–September) still offer fun, smaller waves perfect for beginners and longboarders, with far fewer crowds.
What level do you need for Cathedral?
Cathedral is for intermediate to advanced surfers. It breaks faster and more powerfully than The Bay, over a rock and sand bottom. Confident intermediates can handle it on smaller days (2–3 ft); on bigger swells (4 ft+), it is strictly an advanced wave.
How far is Imsouane from Agadir airport?
About 1 hour and 30 minutes by car (approximately 90 km north of Agadir). Most surf camps include airport transfers in their packages.
Is Imsouane crowded?
The Bay can get busy in peak winter months (December–February) with both surf camps and independent travellers. However, because the wave is so long, the crowd spreads out along the point and sessions remain remarkably civil compared to Taghazout.
What wetsuit do I need for Imsouane?
A 4/3 mm fullsuit in winter (December–March), a 3/2 mm in shoulder seasons (October–November, April–May), and boardshorts or a 2 mm top in summer (June–September). Water temperatures range from 16°C to 23°C.
Are there surf camps in Imsouane?
Yes — Imsouane has over a dozen surf camps catering to all levels, from budget hostels with shared dorms to boutique riads with private rooms. Many camps offer beginner packages with daily lessons, equipment, and meals included.
Can I longboard at Imsouane?
Absolutely — The Bay is considered one of the best longboard waves on the planet. Its slow, peeling face gives longboarders plenty of time for noserides, cross-steps, and style. Many longboarders consider Imsouane a bucket-list destination.
Is there nightlife in Imsouane?
Imsouane is a quiet fishing village, not a party town. There are a handful of cafés and small restaurants, plus sunset gatherings on the cliff overlooking The Bay. For more nightlife, Taghazout (30 minutes south) is livelier.
What's the difference between Imsouane and Taghazout?
Imsouane is smaller, quieter, and more focused on beginners and longboarders, anchored by The Bay's gentle waves. Taghazout is the surf capital with more powerful breaks like Anchor Point, a wider range of restaurants and accommodation, and a bigger international scene.
