Visa-Free Entry — Who Qualifies

Morocco has one of the most accessible visa policies in Africa. Citizens of over 70 countries can enter Morocco without a visa for tourism purposes. This includes all EU member states, the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, and many others. You simply receive an entry stamp in your passport when you pass through immigration at the airport.

The standard visa-free period is 90 days. This is a rolling 90-day window within any 180-day period — meaning you can stay in Morocco for up to 90 days, leave, and return after another 90 days for a further stay. For a typical surf trip of 1-2 weeks, you will not come close to this limit.

If your nationality is not on the visa-free list, you will need to apply for a tourist visa at your nearest Moroccan embassy or consulate before travelling. The process typically takes 1-4 weeks and requires a passport photo, completed application form, flight bookings, accommodation confirmation, and proof of sufficient funds.

Passport Validity Requirements

This is the most common entry problem for surf travellers. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned date of entry into Morocco. This rule is strictly enforced — not by Moroccan immigration (which can be flexible) but by airlines, who will deny boarding at your departure airport if your passport does not meet the requirement.

Check Your Passport NOW

We have seen guests turned away at check-in because their passport had 5 months and 3 weeks of validity remaining. The 6-month rule is applied rigidly by budget airlines. If your passport expires within 8 months of your planned trip, renew it now — passport offices can take weeks during peak periods.

Other passport requirements: your passport must be in good physical condition (no significant damage, water exposure, or torn pages), it must have at least one blank page for the entry stamp, and if you hold dual citizenship, enter and exit Morocco on the same passport.

Bringing Surf Equipment Through Customs

The good news: Moroccan customs are relaxed about personal sports equipment. Bringing a surfboard (or two in a travel bag), wetsuits, and accessories poses no issues. There are no special duties, taxes, or declaration requirements for personal surf equipment.

The only exception: if you are carrying multiple brand-new boards (3+) still in original packaging, customs may suspect you intend to sell them commercially. If you genuinely need a large quiver for a long trip, bring purchase receipts or invoices to demonstrate the boards are for personal use. For a typical surfer with one or two boards in a travel bag, this is never an issue.

Random customs checks do occur at Agadir airport. If selected, officers will ask you to open your board bag. This is routine and takes 2 minutes — simply unzip the bag, show the contents, and you will be waved through. Moroccan customs officers are familiar with surf tourism and generally friendly.

SIM Cards & Staying Connected

Staying connected in Morocco is easy and affordable. The three main mobile networks — Maroc Telecom, Orange, and Inwi — all offer tourist SIM cards with generous data packages. You will find their kiosks in the arrivals hall at Agadir airport, open for all arriving flights.

A typical tourist SIM costs approximately 100 MAD (â‚¬9) and includes 10 GB of data, plus local calls and texts. Activation takes about 5 minutes — the agent will need your passport for registration (this is a legal requirement in Morocco). Top-ups are available at any corner shop (hanout), supermarket, or via the provider's app.

Coverage is excellent in Taghazout, Tamraght, and along the coast. Imsouane has good coverage in the village but patchy signal at the more remote surf spots. 4G speeds are generally reliable. If you plan to work remotely, Maroc Telecom typically offers the best overall coverage in rural areas.

Currency — The Moroccan Dirham

The Moroccan Dirham (MAD, sometimes written as DH) is a closed currency, meaning you cannot obtain it outside Morocco. You must exchange money or withdraw Dirhams after arrival. As of mid-2026, the exchange rate is approximately 10.5-11 MAD to 1 euro.

How to get Dirhams: ATMs at Agadir airport (in the arrivals hall) accept international cards and dispense Dirhams at the official exchange rate. This is the best option — withdraw enough for a few days (approximately 2,000-3,000 MAD for a couple for a week of incidentals). There are also ATMs in Taghazout and Tamraght.

Cards vs cash: Visa and Mastercard are accepted at surf camps, larger restaurants, and supermarkets. Small cafes, surf shops, market stalls, and taxis are cash-only. Always carry some Dirhams. Euros are accepted in tourist areas but at a bad rate — use them only as an emergency backup.

Currency Tip

Notify your bank of your travel dates before leaving. Moroccan ATM withdrawals can trigger fraud alerts on some cards. Also check your bank's foreign transaction fees — some charge 2-3% per transaction. A fee-free travel card (Revolut, Wise, Monzo, N26) saves money on a week-long trip.

Travel Insurance Requirements

Morocco does not legally require travel insurance for entry. However, travelling without it is a gamble you should not take. Here is what your policy must cover:

  • Surfing coverage: Many standard travel insurance policies exclude "hazardous activities," which often includes surfing. Check the fine print and add an adventure-sports rider if needed. Companies like World Nomads, True Traveller, and SportsCoverDirect specialise in surf travel insurance.
  • Medical treatment and hospitalisation: Private clinics in Agadir provide good care, but you pay upfront without insurance. A broken bone or stitches for a reef cut can cost hundreds of euros.
  • Emergency medical evacuation: If you suffer a serious injury requiring repatriation to Europe, the cost without insurance can exceed â‚¬20,000.
  • Sports equipment: Check if your policy covers loss, theft, or damage to your surfboard and equipment.

Health & Vaccinations

No vaccinations are legally required for entry to Morocco. The standard recommendation from travel health authorities is to be up to date on routine vaccines: tetanus (particularly relevant for surfers due to reef cuts), hepatitis A, hepatitis B, typhoid (if eating outside major tourist restaurants), and diphtheria.

Tap water in Morocco is technically treated, but avoid drinking it. Stick to bottled or filtered water. Most surf camps provide filtered drinking water for guests. Bring anti-diarrhoea medication and rehydration salts as a precaution.

Arrival at the Airport — What to Expect

Arriving at Agadir airport is straightforward. The process typically takes 30-45 minutes from landing to exiting the terminal:

  1. Disembark and follow signs to immigration. Have your passport and completed landing card ready.
  2. At the immigration desk, the officer will check your passport, stamp it with the entry date, and may ask about the purpose and length of your stay. Answer "tourism" or "surfing" — this is routine.
  3. Collect your checked luggage and board bag from the baggage carousel. Surfboard bags usually come out on the oversized luggage belt.
  4. Pass through customs (the green "nothing to declare" channel). If selected for a random bag check, open your board bag for inspection.
  5. Exit into the arrivals hall. Your camp driver or pre-booked transfer will be waiting with a sign. SIM card kiosks, ATMs, and currency exchange are in this hall.

Pre-Travel Documents Checklist

  • Passport valid for 6+ months beyond your entry date to Morocco
  • At least one blank page in passport for entry stamp
  • Travel insurance certificate (printed + digital copy) — check surfing is covered
  • Flight booking confirmation (printed or offline on phone)
  • Surf camp or accommodation booking confirmation with address
  • Photocopy of passport (kept separate from actual passport)
  • Bank card + notify bank of Morocco travel dates
  • Cash in euros (backup) — withdraw Dirhams from ATM on arrival
  • Driving licence + International Driving Permit (if renting a car)
  • Prescription medications in original packaging with copy of prescription

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Morocco as an EU citizen?
No. Citizens of EU countries, the UK, the USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and many other nations can enter Morocco visa-free for stays of up to 90 days. You simply receive an entry stamp upon arrival.
How long can I stay in Morocco without a visa?
90 days for most nationalities including all EU countries, UK, USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, and South Korea.
What is the passport validity requirement for Morocco?
Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your planned date of entry. This is strictly enforced by airlines, who will deny boarding if your passport is close to expiry.
Can I bring my surfboard through Moroccan customs?
Yes. Surfboards, wetsuits, and standard surf equipment pass through Moroccan customs without issue. There are no special duties or taxes on personal sports equipment.
Do I need travel insurance for Morocco?
Morocco does not legally require travel insurance for entry, but you should absolutely have it. Ensure your policy covers surfing, medical evacuation, and sports equipment.
What currency is used in Morocco?
The Moroccan Dirham (MAD). As of mid-2026, 1 euro equals approximately 10.5-11 MAD. Withdraw Dirhams from ATMs for the best rate.
Can I get a SIM card at Agadir airport?
Yes. Maroc Telecom, Orange, and Inwi all have kiosks in the arrivals hall. A tourist SIM with 10 GB of data costs approximately 100 MAD (â‚¬9).
Can I use my mobile phone in Morocco?
Yes. EU roaming regulations generally do not apply to Morocco. A local SIM card is significantly cheaper than roaming.
Are there any COVID-19 entry restrictions for Morocco?
As of mid-2026, Morocco has no COVID-19 testing or vaccination requirements for entry. Check current regulations within 7 days of travel.
What do I need to declare at Moroccan customs?
You must declare amounts over 100,000 MAD in cash (or equivalent). Personal effects, including surf equipment, do not need to be declared.
How do I fill in the Morocco entry form on the plane?
On your flight, you will receive a simple landing card. Fill in your name, passport number, flight number, accommodation address, and purpose of visit (tourism). Keep it ready for immigration.
Do children need their own passport for Morocco?
Yes. Every traveller, including infants, must have their own valid passport. Children listed on a parent's passport are no longer accepted. Ensure each family member's passport meets the 6-month validity requirement.
