Flying to Agadir — Direct Flights from Europe

Agadir Al-Massira Airport (AGA) is the gateway to Morocco's surf coast. Located 25 km southeast of Agadir city and 45 km south of Taghazout, it receives direct flights from dozens of European cities — making it one of the most accessible surf destinations from the continent.

When to book: Winter flights (November–March) to Agadir sell out quickly — this is peak Moroccan surf season. Book at least 6–8 weeks ahead for the best fares. Summer flights (June–September) are less busy and often cheaper. Expect to pay â‚¬50–â‚¬150 each way from European hubs, with the best deals typically found on Ryanair and easyJet if booked early.

Pro Tip

Add your board bag when you book your flight — do not wait. Airlines allocate a limited number of sports-equipment slots per flight, and both the price and availability are significantly better at the time of booking versus check-in.

Agadir Airport to Taghazout — All Transfer Options

Once you land in Agadir, you have four main options to reach Taghazout. Here they are ranked from best to worst:

1. Surf Camp Transfer (Best Option)

If you have booked a surf camp, your airport transfer is almost certainly included. A driver will be waiting in the arrivals hall with a sign bearing your name or the camp's name. They will load your boards, drive you directly to your accommodation in 45 minutes, and often provide water and a briefing on the way. This is the most seamless option and one of the key reasons to book a camp.

2. Pre-Booked Private Transfer (Recommended for Independent Travellers)

Private transfers cost â‚¬25–â‚¬35 and can be booked online before you travel (GetYourGuide, Viator, or directly through your accommodation). The driver meets you at arrivals with your name on a sign, helps with luggage, and drives you door-to-door. This is significantly better value than an airport taxi and eliminates the need to negotiate after a long flight.

3. Airport Taxi (Acceptable but Overpriced)

Official airport taxis are available outside the terminal. Expect to pay 300–400 MAD (â‚¬30–â‚¬40) for the 45-minute trip to Taghazout. Always agree the price before getting in — airport taxis do not use meters for long-distance trips. The driver will likely quote 400–500 MAD initially; negotiate to 300–350 MAD. Taxis can fit 3–4 passengers plus luggage but may struggle with multiple board bags.

4. Public Bus (Not Recommended)

A public bus runs from the airport to Agadir city centre (Inezgane), where you would need to transfer to a local bus or grand taxi to Taghazout. Total journey time: 2+ hours. This is not practical with surf bags and is not recommended for surf travellers.

Avoid the Tout Trap

At Agadir airport arrivals, you will be approached by unofficial "helpers" offering taxi rides and luggage assistance. Politely decline and walk directly to the official taxi rank or your pre-booked driver. These touts charge inflated prices and are not licensed. Your camp driver or pre-booked transfer will be holding a sign — look for that, not for someone approaching you.

Driving from Marrakech

Marrakech Menara Airport (RAK) is a viable alternative gateway, particularly for travellers from cities with limited Agadir connections. The drive from Marrakech to Taghazout takes approximately 3 hours via the A7 motorway.

The route: The A7 is a modern toll motorway that runs from Marrakech southwest to Agadir. The road surface is excellent, the signage is clear (in Arabic and French), and there are service stations with fuel, food, and toilets approximately every 50 km. The drive passes through the High Atlas foothills — the scenery is stunning, particularly the stretch between Chichaoua and Imintanoute where the mountains frame the highway.

Transfer options from Marrakech: Private transfers cost â‚¬100–â‚¬140 and take approximately 3 hours. If you are arriving on a late flight, consider spending a night in Marrakech and travelling the next day — the motorway is safe but driving in the dark in an unfamiliar country adds stress. Most surf camps offer Marrakech transfers for an additional fee (typically â‚¬80–â‚¬120).

Toll costs: The A7 motorway toll from Marrakech to Agadir is approximately 70 MAD (â‚¬6.50). Tolls are paid in cash at booths along the route. Keep small notes and coins handy.

Public Transport Options

Morocco's public transport network is extensive and cheap, but it is not designed for surf travellers with large bags. Here is an honest assessment:

  • CTM and Supratours buses: These are Morocco's main intercity bus companies, offering comfortable, air-conditioned coaches between major cities. Agadir to Taghazout is technically possible via a local bus but impractical. CTM is excellent for longer journeys (e.g. Marrakech to Agadir, approx. 120 MAD / â‚¬11) but will not get you from Agadir city to Taghazout easily with surf gear.
  • Grands taxis: Shared Mercedes sedans that run fixed routes between towns. From Agadir to Taghazout, grand taxis depart from the Inezgane taxi rank and cost approximately 30–40 MAD per person. They wait until the car is full (6 passengers) before departing. With surf bags, this is uncomfortable and may cost extra. A private grand taxi (you pay for all seats) costs around 200 MAD and is faster.
  • Local buses: Bus #32 runs from Agadir city to Taghazout and Tamraght. It costs pennies but is slow, crowded, and you will struggle with surf equipment. Not recommended.

Getting Around Taghazout, Tamraght & Imsouane

Once you are on the ground, getting around is straightforward:

  • Taghazout: The village is compact and entirely walkable. Cafés, restaurants, surf shops, the beach, Hash Point, and Panorama Point are all within a 10–15 minute walk. The main road through town has everything you need.
  • Taghazout to Tamraght: These neighbouring villages are 5–10 minutes apart by car. A petit taxi costs 20–30 MAD. Many people walk the coastal path between them — it is a pleasant 30–40 minute walk with ocean views.
  • Taghazout to Imsouane: Imsouane is 85 km north (about 1 hour 15 minutes). Grand taxis run this route for approximately 300–400 MAD. Most surf camps offer day trips to Imsouane when conditions align, which is the easiest option.
  • Petit taxis: Small blue cars (in the Agadir region) that operate within and between nearby towns. They are metered for short trips but you will often need to agree a fare for longer distances. A trip within Taghazout costs 10–20 MAD. Taghazout to Tamraght costs 20–30 MAD. Always agree the fare before getting in if the driver does not turn on the meter.

Rental Cars — Pros, Cons & Tips

The question of whether to rent a car divides opinion among Morocco surf travellers. Here is the honest breakdown:

Pros: Total freedom. You can chase swell independently, visit multiple spots in one day, explore the coast beyond the main breaks, and make side trips to Paradise Valley or Essaouira. If you are surfing independently (not with a camp), a car is a significant advantage. It also gives you flexibility for food shopping, evening excursions, and day trips on flat days.

Cons: It is an additional cost (â‚¬25–â‚¬40/day for a basic car, more for something that can carry boards). Moroccan driving requires confidence — road rules are loosely observed, overtaking on blind corners is common, and roundabouts operate on a "might is right" principle. Parking in Taghazout is limited, especially in winter. If your surf camp includes daily transport, a rental car is largely redundant.

If you do rent: Book with an international company (Hertz, Europcar, Avis) at Agadir airport. Take photos of every scratch before driving away — damage disputes are common. Ensure the car has fold-down rear seats or roof racks for boards. Full insurance with zero excess is worth the extra cost. An International Driving Permit is recommended alongside your home licence, though many rental agencies will accept a standard EU/UK licence.

Our Recommendation

If you book a surf camp with daily transport, do not rent a car. The camp's drivers know exactly which spots are working each day, saving you hours of driving between breaks to check conditions. If you are travelling independently, a rental car becomes much more valuable — rent a small SUV or estate car with fold-down seats that can fit a board bag inside, as roof racks on rental cars are rare in Morocco.

Surf Camp Transfers — What Is Included

Almost every surf camp in the Taghazout area includes airport transfers from Agadir (AGA) in their package price. Here is what you can typically expect:

  • Pickup: A driver meets you at Agadir airport arrivals with a sign bearing your name or the camp's name.
  • Vehicle: A minibus or large 4x4 with space for passengers and board bags. The driver handles all luggage.
  • Journey: 45 minutes door-to-door. The driver often provides water and a brief orientation — pointing out surf spots visible from the road, recommending cafés, and answering first-day questions.
  • Return: On your departure day, the camp arranges your return transfer to the airport, timed to arrive 2 hours before your flight.
  • Marrakech transfers: Usually available for an additional fee (â‚¬80–â‚¬120). Book this when you reserve your camp.

Your Arrival Day — Step by Step

Before You Fly

Confirm your transfer or surf camp pickup. Save the driver's WhatsApp number. Download offline maps of the Taghazout area on Google Maps. Have your accommodation address written in Arabic or French to show a taxi driver if needed.

At Agadir Airport

Collect your bags and board bag. Walk past the touts to the arrivals hall exit. Look for your name on a sign. If using a taxi, walk to the official rank outside — do not accept rides from anyone approaching you inside the terminal.

45-Minute Drive

The N1 coastal road runs north past Tamraght, Aourir (Banana Village), and into Taghazout. On a clear day, you will see the Atlantic and surf spots from the road. Your driver will point out landmarks.

Arriving in Taghazout

Settle into your accommodation. Your camp host or riad manager will orient you — where to eat, where the surf shop is, what time the morning surf session starts. Unpack your boards or visit the camp's rental quiver to select your board for the next morning.

First Evening

Walk to the Taghazout viewpoint to watch the sunset over Anchor Point. Find a rooftop café for mint tea. Get an early night — tomorrow the surfing begins.

Travel Day Checklist

  • Passport (valid 6+ months beyond return)
  • Flight booking with board bag added (if bringing your own)
  • Transfer or surf camp pickup confirmation + driver's WhatsApp
  • Accommodation address (in English and French/Arabic)
  • Offline Google Maps of Taghazout area downloaded
  • Cash in Euros (backup) and Dirhams (for taxi/small purchases)
  • Travel insurance documents (printed)
  • Phone with Moroccan SIM or roaming activated
  • Charged power bank (for the journey)
  • Light jacket or fleece for the airport/plane

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the nearest airport to Taghazout?
Agadir Al-Massira Airport (AGA) is the nearest airport, located approximately 45 minutes by car from Taghazout. It is the primary gateway for surf trips to Morocco's Atlantic coast, with direct flights from dozens of European cities.
Which airlines fly direct to Agadir?
Ryanair, easyJet, Wizz Air, Transavia, Royal Air Maroc, TUI fly, and Air France all operate direct or seasonal routes to Agadir from major European cities including London, Paris, Warsaw, Berlin, Amsterdam, Barcelona, Brussels, Dublin, and Manchester. Route availability changes seasonally.
How do I get from Agadir airport to Taghazout?
Options: (1) Pre-booked private transfer — â‚¬25–â‚¬35, 45 min, door-to-door, recommended. (2) Airport taxi — 300–400 MAD (â‚¬30–â‚¬40), agree price before getting in. (3) Surf camp transfer — usually included in your package, this is the best option. (4) Bus — cheapest but slow, requires a change in Agadir city, not recommended with surf bags.
Can I drive from Marrakech to Taghazout?
Yes, the drive from Marrakech Menara Airport (RAK) to Taghazout takes approximately 3 hours via the A7 motorway. The road is excellent — a modern, well-maintained toll motorway — and the drive passes through the High Atlas foothills before descending to the Atlantic coast.
Do surf camps provide airport transfers?
Yes, the vast majority of surf camps in Taghazout, Tamraght, and Imsouane include airport transfers from Agadir (AGA) in their package price. Some camps also offer transfers from Marrakech (RAK) for an additional fee. Always confirm this when booking — it is one of the key benefits of booking a camp.
How do I get around Taghazout without a car?
Taghazout village is entirely walkable. Everything — cafés, restaurants, shops, surf rental, and several surf spots — is within a 10–15 minute walk. For spots further afield, surf camps provide daily transport. Petit taxis connect Taghazout to Tamraght (10 min, ~30 MAD).
Should I rent a car in Morocco for a surf trip?
Renting a car gives you freedom to chase swell and explore independently. However, most surf camps include daily transport to the best breaks, making a rental car unnecessary. If you do rent, book with an international company at the airport, photograph every scratch before driving away, and ensure the car can accommodate board bags.
How long is the transfer from Agadir to Taghazout?
45 minutes by car via the N1 coastal road. The route is scenic, passing through Tamraght and Aourir before arriving in Taghazout. Traffic is rarely an issue except during peak holiday periods (August, Eid holidays).
Is there public transport from Agadir to Taghazout?
Yes, but it is not convenient with surf bags. You would need to take an airport taxi or bus to Agadir's city centre (Inezgane bus station), then catch a local bus (#32) or grand taxi to Taghazout. This takes 2+ hours and is not recommended. A private transfer costs â‚¬25–â‚¬35 and takes 45 minutes door-to-door.
How do I get from Taghazout to Imsouane?
Imsouane is approximately 85 km north of Taghazout, about 1 hour 15 minutes by car. Options: surf camp transport (if your camp services Imsouane — most do), grand taxi from the Taghazout rank (negotiate fare, expect ~300–400 MAD), or rental car. There is no direct bus. Many camps run day trips to Imsouane when conditions are right.
Is Uber available in Morocco?
No, Uber and similar ride-hailing apps do not operate in Morocco. The local equivalent is Careem (limited availability in Agadir) or local taxi apps. Petit taxis and grand taxis are the standard. Always agree the fare before getting in.
How much does a taxi cost from Agadir airport to Taghazout?
Official airport taxis charge 300–400 MAD (â‚¬30–â‚¬40) for the 45-minute trip to Taghazout. Pre-booked private transfers cost â‚¬25–â‚¬35 and offer better value plus the peace of mind of a driver waiting with your name on a sign. Surf camp transfers are typically included in your package at no extra cost.
