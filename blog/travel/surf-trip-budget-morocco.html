Budget Overview — What to Expect

Morocco is one of the best-value surf destinations in the world for European travellers. A one-week surf trip can cost anywhere from â‚¬400 on a shoestring budget to â‚¬1,500+ for a premium all-inclusive experience. The sweet spot — booking a quality all-inclusive surf camp with flights — typically lands between â‚¬600 and â‚¬900 per person.

The biggest single cost is your flight, which can range from â‚¬50 to â‚¬300 return depending on where you are flying from, when you book, and the season. After that, your daily costs in Morocco are remarkably low compared to European surf destinations. A fresh-squeezed orange juice costs â‚¬1. A hearty tagine dinner costs â‚¬5-â‚¬8. A taxi ride across town costs â‚¬2-â‚¬3. These low daily costs are what make Morocco so attractive for week-long surf trips.

Quick Reality Check

A one-week surf trip to Morocco — including flights, accommodation, food, surf equipment, and coaching — typically costs less than a long weekend surfing in southwest France or Cornwall. The value proposition is exceptional.

Surf Camp Packages — The Best Value

For most surf travellers, an all-inclusive surf camp offers the best value in Morocco. At â‚¬550-â‚¬750 per person per week, you get accommodation, daily breakfast and dinner (often lunch too), 2 guided surf sessions per day with coaching, unlimited board and wetsuit use, daily transport to the best breaks, and airport transfers from Agadir.

If you were to book all of these services independently, the cost would be roughly the same — but without the convenience, the local knowledge, the social aspect of being part of a camp, and the guarantee that you are surfing the right spot on the right tide every day. Surf camps achieve these prices through economies of scale — group meals, shared transport, and bulk equipment purchases.

"When guests calculate what they would have spent independently — accommodation, meals, board rental, a couple of lessons, daily taxis to different spots — they almost always find the camp package was the better deal. And they did not have to think about a single logistical detail all week."

— SurfAgencyMorocco operations team

Transport & Getting Around

Local transport costs are modest. If you are at a surf camp, daily transport to the breaks is included. For independent travellers:

  • Airport transfer (Agadir to Taghazout): â‚¬25-â‚¬35 private, â‚¬30-â‚¬40 taxi. Included with most surf camps.
  • Petit taxi (within Taghazout): 10-20 MAD (â‚¬1-â‚¬2) per trip.
  • Petit taxi (Taghazout to Tamraght): 20-30 MAD (â‚¬2-â‚¬3).
  • Grand taxi (Taghazout to Imsouane): 300-400 MAD (â‚¬28-â‚¬38) for the whole car.
  • Rental car: â‚¬25-â‚¬40/day plus fuel and insurance.
  • Daily surf spot transport (if not at a camp): Budget â‚¬10-â‚¬15/day if splitting taxi costs with others.

The Mid-Range Sweet Spot

At â‚¬600-â‚¬900 total for a week, the surf camp model delivers the best experience-to-cost ratio. You get daily coaching, transport to the best spots, quality equipment, meals, and the social atmosphere of a camp — for roughly the same total cost as organising everything independently. This is why 80% of our guests return.

Solo vs Couple vs Group Budgets

Per-person costs decrease as your group size increases, primarily due to shared accommodation:

  • Solo traveller (surf camp): â‚¬700-â‚¬1,000 total per week. Solo travellers typically pay a single supplement for a private room or share a twin room with another guest.
  • Couple (surf camp): â‚¬500-â‚¬800 per person per week. Sharing a private double room reduces the per-person cost of accommodation.
  • Group of 4+ (surf camp): â‚¬450-â‚¬700 per person per week. Many camps offer group discounts. Sharing a multi-bed apartment or riad suite brings per-person accommodation costs down further.
  • Independent group (self-organised): Renting a whole apartment or riad (â‚¬40-â‚¬80/night divided by 4 = â‚¬10-â‚¬20/night each) plus shared taxi costs makes independent travel very affordable in groups.

Money-Saving Tips

  • Book flights early (6-8 weeks ahead): The single biggest saving you can make. Last-minute winter flights to Agadir can exceed â‚¬400.
  • Travel in shoulder season (Oct, Apr-May): Good waves, lower flight prices, fewer crowds. The best value window.
  • Book a surf camp: The camp model bundles all costs at a price that is very hard to beat independently.
  • Do not bring a board bag: Save â‚¬120-â‚¬180 in airline fees by using camp rental boards. Unless you are an advanced surfer with specific equipment needs, this is almost always the right call.
  • Fly midweek: Tuesday and Wednesday flights are often â‚¬30-â‚¬50 cheaper than weekend departures.
  • Use a fee-free travel card: Revolut, Wise, Monzo, and N26 offer near-perfect exchange rates with no foreign transaction fees. Standard bank cards can add 2-3% to every transaction.
  • Eat local: A café lunch costs â‚¬3-â‚¬6. A tourist-oriented restaurant costs â‚¬12-â‚¬18. The food at local places is often better, too.
  • Bring sunscreen from home: Quality zinc sunscreen in Morocco is expensive and hard to find.

Budget Planning Timeline

8 Weeks Before

Set flight price alerts. Research surf camps and check what is included in each package. Start saving — set aside â‚¬100-â‚¬200 per week if you are building up to the trip.

6 Weeks Before

Book flights (add board bag if needed). Book surf camp and pay deposit. Open a fee-free travel card (Revolut, Wise) if you do not already have one.

2 Weeks Before

Pay remaining camp balance. Buy travel insurance. Notify your bank of travel dates. Load your travel card with euros for ATM withdrawals.

1 Week Before

Withdraw a small amount of euros for backup cash. Pack your bag — shop for anything missing (sunscreen, medical supplies). Check passport validity.

Budget Planning Checklist

  • Flights booked (return) + board bag added if bringing your own
  • Surf camp or accommodation booked + deposit paid
  • Travel insurance purchased (check surfing is covered)
  • Fee-free travel card set up and loaded with euros
  • Bank notified of Morocco travel dates
  • Daily spending budget calculated (â‚¬15-â‚¬25/day for incidentals)
  • Backup euros in cash (â‚¬50-â‚¬100 for emergencies)
  • Sunscreen, medical kit, and toiletries purchased (do not rely on buying in Morocco)
  • Passport checked — valid 6+ months beyond return date

Return flights from European hubs to Agadir range from â‚¬50-â‚¬300 depending on the season, how early you book, and your departure city. Winter flights are more expensive (â‚¬150-â‚¬300). Add â‚¬60-â‚¬90 each way for a board bag.

If your surf camp includes meals: â‚¬10-â‚¬20/day for lunch, snacks, drinks, and incidentals. If self-catering: â‚¬25-â‚¬40/day for all meals, transport, and incidentals.

Fly Ryanair to Agadir in summer (â‚¬50-â‚¬80 return). Stay in a hostel (â‚¬15-â‚¬25/night). Eat local (â‚¬10-â‚¬15/day). Rent a board independently (â‚¬50-â‚¬70/week). Total: approximately â‚¬300-â‚¬400/week.

Pay in Moroccan Dirhams (MAD) whenever possible. While euros are accepted in tourist areas, the exchange rate applied is always unfavourable. Withdraw Dirhams from ATMs for the best rate.

Tipping is customary but not mandatory. In restaurants, 5-10% is standard. Round up taxi fares. For surf camp staff, a tip of â‚¬20-â‚¬50 per guest per week is appreciated.

A private 2-hour surf lesson in Taghazout or Tamraght costs approximately â‚¬30-â‚¬50. Group lessons (included in camp packages) offer better value.

Can I use my credit card everywhere in Taghazout?
No. Surf camps, hotels, and sit-down restaurants accept cards. Small cafés, surf shops, street food stalls, and taxis are cash-only. Always carry Dirhams.
