Why Book a Surf Camp in Morocco?

Morocco has one of the highest concentrations of surf camps in the world. The stretch of coast between Agadir and Imsouane — barely 80 kilometres — is home to hundreds of camps, schools, and surf-oriented guesthouses. The reason is simple: consistent waves year-round, affordable prices compared to Europe, and a welcoming culture that embraces travelling surfers.

Booking a surf camp rather than going solo offers distinct advantages. Camps provide daily transport to the best breaks based on conditions — something that is almost impossible to replicate on your own without extensive local knowledge. They supply quality boards and wetsuits, eliminating the headache of airline board fees and gear logistics. They connect you with local guides who know every rock, every tide, and every face in every lineup. And they offer a social experience — shared meals, rooftop sunsets, and the camaraderie of a group who share your obsession with waves.

8 Key Factors to Consider

1. Your Surf Level

This is the most important filter. A camp that excels at teaching beginners may bore an advanced surfer to tears. Conversely, an advanced camp will not teach you to pop up. Be honest about your level — "intermediate" means different things to different camps. Describe your actual experience: "I can paddle out back, catch green waves unassisted, and ride down the line" is clearer than "intermediate".

2. Location — Taghazout vs Tamraght vs Imsouane

Each village has a distinct character. See the comparison table below for a full breakdown.

3. Group Size & Instructor Ratio

The single biggest determinant of how much you will improve. A ratio of 4–6 surfers per instructor is ideal. 8 per instructor is acceptable. Anything above 10 means you are getting crowd control, not coaching. Ask explicitly: "What is your maximum group size per instructor?"

4. Coaching Quality

Not all surf instructors are created equal. Local Moroccan guides who have surfed these breaks their entire lives bring knowledge that no international instructor can match. Look for camps that employ ISA or locally certified instructors, offer video analysis, and conduct both in-water coaching and theory sessions. Beware of camps where the "instructor" is just a strong surfer with no teaching qualifications.

5. Accommodation Style

Options range from shared dorm rooms in surf hostels (â‚¬20–30/night) to private rooms in traditional riads (â‚¬40–80/night) to luxury villas with pools and rooftop yoga decks (â‚¬80–150+/night). Decide whether you want a social atmosphere or privacy, and whether a rooftop terrace with ocean views matters to you.

6. Food Quality

Moroccan cuisine is one of the great pleasures of a surf trip here. The best camps serve home-cooked tagines, fresh salads, grilled fish, and hearty breakfasts with msemen (Moroccan pancakes), eggs, and fresh fruit. Ask if the camp accommodates dietary requirements. A camp that serves the same couscous five nights a week is cutting corners.

7. Transport to Spots

This is non-negotiable. Any decent surf camp includes daily transport to different breaks based on the day's conditions. A camp that takes guests to the same beach every day regardless of swell, wind, and tide is not a real surf camp — it is a guesthouse near a beach. Confirm: "Do you choose the daily spot based on conditions, or do you always go to the same beach?"

8. Seasonal Timing

The camp you choose should match the season you are visiting. Winter camps (Dec–Feb) should be equipped for cold water (4/3mm wetsuits) and big swell. Summer camps (May–Sep) should offer lighter wetsuits and focus on smaller-wave coaching. Spring and autumn are the most versatile.

Pro Tip from Our Guides

If you are torn between Taghazout and Tamraght, know this: they are only 10 minutes apart by car. Many camps in Tamraght take guests to Taghazout breaks when conditions are right, and vice versa. The real question is where you want to wake up: amidst the buzz of Taghazout or the tranquillity of a hillside Tamraght terrace.

Camps by Surfing Level

  • Complete Beginners (<1 year): Prioritise safety, small groups, and patient instructors. Choose camps that use foam boards on sand-bottomed beaches (Imsouane Bay or Banana Beach). Avoid point-break camps — you are not ready for Anchor Point.
  • Improving Intermediates (1–3 years): Look for camps offering video analysis and technique-focused coaching. You want instructors who can help you progress beyond the whitewater — reading waves, generating speed, and basic turns. Tamraght and Taghazout camps work well.
  • Confident Intermediates (3–5 years): You are ready for point breaks on smaller days. Choose camps that offer a mix of beach and point break sessions, and that include transport to Anchor Point and Killer Point when conditions suit your level.
  • Advanced (5+ years, comfortable overhead): Seek out camps that specifically advertise advanced or progressive coaching. You want guided dawn patrols at premier breaks, video analysis, and instructors who can critique technique at a high level.

Red Flags — What to Avoid

  • No conditions-based spot selection. If the camp always goes to the same beach regardless of swell, it is not a surf camp — it is a hotel near a beach.
  • Vague or non-existent group size information. If the website does not tell you the maximum students per instructor, it is probably too high.
  • No mention of instructor qualifications. "Experienced surfer" is not the same as "qualified surf instructor".
  • Prices that seem too good to be true. A week of accommodation, food, coaching, equipment, and transport for â‚¬199? Corners are being cut — probably on everything.
  • No photos of the actual accommodation. Stock images of Moroccan riads do not count. If they will not show you where you will sleep, walk away.
  • No recent reviews. Every camp has some negative reviews — that is normal. A total absence of reviews, or only suspiciously glowing ones, is a warning sign.
Important Warning

In recent years, "fake surf camps" have appeared — essentially unlicensed operators renting a house and calling it a surf camp. Always verify that the camp has a registered business, liability insurance, and qualified instructors. A legitimate camp will provide this information willingly.

Questions to Ask Before Booking

  1. "What is the maximum number of surfers per instructor?"
  2. "Do you choose the daily surf spot based on conditions, or is it fixed?"
  3. "What qualification do your instructors hold?"
  4. "Are you a registered business with liability insurance?"
  5. "Can you send me photos of the exact room I would stay in?"
  6. "What is included in the price, and what costs extra?"
  7. "Is airport transfer included?"
  8. "What is your cancellation policy?"
  9. "Do you have surfboards suitable for my level and size/weight?"
  10. "Can you put me in touch with a recent guest who surfed at my level?"

Best Season to Book Each Type of Camp

  • Beginner Camps: May–September (warm water, small waves, lowest prices) or March–April/October–November (mild weather, manageable waves, good value).
  • Intermediate Camps: October–November and March–April are ideal — consistent swell without the full winter power, pleasant weather, and fewer crowds than peak winter.
  • Advanced Camps: December–February for the biggest swells and best point-break conditions. Book 2–3 months ahead.
  • Best Value Overall: March–April and October–November. You get 80% of the wave quality of winter at 60–70% of the price, with far fewer crowds.

Solo Travellers, Couples & Groups

  • Solo travellers: Most Moroccan surf camps are built around the solo traveller experience. Communal meals, shared rooms, and group surf sessions make it incredibly easy to meet people. Some camps now offer private rooms at a small supplement.
  • Couples: Look for camps with private double rooms, ideally with en-suite bathrooms and perhaps a rooftop terrace. Taghazout riads with ocean views are especially romantic. Confirm the room type before booking.
  • Groups (4+): Many camps offer group discounts. You may be able to negotiate a private instructor for your group if you book together. Groups should spread out in the lineup — do not dominate a single peak.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a surf camp in Morocco cost?
Moroccan surf camps range from budget hostels at â‚¬250–400 per week to premium riads at â‚¬650–1,200 per week. Most mid-range camps (â‚¬450–750 per week) include accommodation, daily surf coaching, equipment rental, transport to breaks, and most meals. Compared to European surf destinations, Morocco offers excellent value for money — a week here often costs half what you would pay in Portugal or France for equivalent quality.
Which is better for a surf camp: Taghazout, Tamraght, or Imsouane?
It depends on your priorities. Taghazout is the classic choice — famous lineups, great cafés, nightlife, and the iconic Anchor Point backdrop. Tamraght offers a quieter, more authentic village atmosphere with equally good surf and slightly lower prices. Imsouane is the go-to for longboarders and beginners who want the safety of a sand-bottomed bay. For variety, many experienced surfers base themselves in Taghazout or Tamraght and make day trips to Imsouane when the swell is right.
What should I look for in a surf camp in Morocco?
Look for: qualified, experienced local guides (not just strong surfers — good teachers); small group sizes (6–8 max per instructor); daily transport to the best breaks (not fixed to one beach); quality equipment included in the price; video analysis if you want to improve; transparent pricing with no hidden extras; and real, recent guest reviews. Red flags include: no transport included, groups larger than 10 per instructor, no mention of guide qualifications, and prices that seem too good to be true.
Is food included in Moroccan surf camps?
Most Moroccan surf camps include full board (breakfast, packed lunch or beach lunch, and dinner) in their package price. The food is typically traditional Moroccan cuisine — tagine, couscous, fresh salads, msemen (Moroccan pancakes), and fish dishes. Dietary requirements (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free) can usually be accommodated if you notify the camp in advance. Some budget camps offer self-catering options instead.
Do surf camps in Morocco include airport transfers?
Most mid-range and premium surf camps include Agadir (AGA) airport transfers in their package price. Budget camps may charge extra (typically â‚¬15–25 each way). Always confirm this before booking — it should be clearly stated in the package description. The transfer from Agadir airport to Taghazout takes about 45 minutes, to Tamraght about 40 minutes, and to Imsouane about 90 minutes.
What is the best season to book a surf camp in Morocco?
October to April is peak season with the biggest, most consistent swells. December to February is for experienced surfers wanting maximum size. March–April and October–November are the sweet spots — great waves, pleasant weather, and fewer crowds. May to September offers smaller waves ideal for beginners and intermediates, with warm water and the lowest prices. Camp prices are typically 20–30% lower in summer.
How far in advance should I book a surf camp in Morocco?
For winter peak season (December–February), book 2–3 months in advance — the best camps fill up by October. For shoulder season (March–April, October–November), 1–2 months ahead is usually sufficient. For summer (May–September), last-minute bookings are often possible, but the best-value camps still fill up. If you have specific dates or want a particular camp, book early.
Do I need travel insurance for a surf trip to Morocco?
Yes — travel insurance is strongly recommended for any surf trip. Make sure your policy covers surfing (some standard policies exclude it), medical evacuation, and dental treatment (reef cuts are common). World Nomads, True Traveller, and SafetyWing are popular choices among surfers heading to Morocco. Your surf camp may also offer local insurance — ask before booking.
Can I join a surf camp as a solo traveller?
Absolutely. Many surf camps in Morocco cater specifically to solo travellers, with shared accommodation, communal meals, and group surf sessions that make it easy to meet people. Solo travellers make up a significant portion of guests at most Taghazout and Tamraght camps. Some camps even offer private rooms at a small supplement if you want your own space.
What's the difference between a surf camp and a surf school?
A surf school typically offers lessons by the hour or day — you arrange your own accommodation and meals. A surf camp is an all-inclusive package: accommodation, meals, daily coaching, equipment, and transport, usually for a week. Surf camps provide a much more immersive experience and better value if you are staying for more than a few days. In Morocco, camps are far more common than stand-alone schools.
Are there surf camps for advanced surfers in Morocco?
Yes, several camps specialise in intermediate to advanced surfing. These offer guided trips to premier breaks like Anchor Point, Killer Point, and Imsouane Cathedral; video analysis; and coaching focused on technique refinement rather than basics. Look for camps that specifically advertise 'advanced trips' or 'progressive coaching', and check that their guides have experience surfing overhead waves themselves.
What are the red flags to avoid when booking a surf camp in Morocco?
Red flags: prices that seem dramatically cheaper than competitors (corners are being cut somewhere); no photos of the actual accommodation or camp; guides who are not local Moroccans (local knowledge is essential); fixed daily schedule to one beach regardless of conditions; no mention of group sizes or instructor-to-student ratios; no recent reviews or only suspiciously perfect 5-star reviews; and any camp that requires full payment months in advance without a clear cancellation policy.
