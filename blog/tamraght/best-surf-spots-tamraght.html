Banana Beach (Plage Imourane) — Beginner to Intermediate

Banana Beach is the heart of Tamraght's surf scene. This long, sandy crescent stretches for over a kilometre, offering consistent, mellow waves that peel both left and right. The sand bottom is forgiving — wipeouts are soft, and you will not find reef, rocks, or urchins here. It is the perfect learning environment, which is why nearly every surf camp in the region brings its beginner groups to Banana Beach.

Do not dismiss it as purely a beginner wave, though. On a solid winter swell, Banana Beach can produce head-high to overhead peaks with surprising punch. The beach is long enough that you can always find a quieter section away from the surf schools. The north end of the beach tends to have steeper, faster waves; the south end is consistently mellower.

Best conditions: NW to WNW swell, 2–6 ft at 8–14s. All tides, though mid-tide gives the best shape. Light offshore E/SE wind. Works year-round.

Crocodile Beach — Intermediate

Just north of Banana Beach, Crocodile Beach takes its name from the distinctive rock formation on the cliff that resembles a crocodile's snout. This is the spot where Tamraght's intermediate surfers graduate to after mastering Banana Beach. The waves here are steeper, faster, and break closer to shore, offering a more dynamic ride.

Crocodile Beach handles bigger swells better than Banana — when the points are maxing out and Banana is closing out, Crocodile often offers clean, shoulder-to-head-high peaks that are perfect for progression. The sandbank shifts seasonally, so the exact peak location moves around. Watch a few sets before paddling out.

Best conditions: NW to WNW swell, 3–8 ft at 10–14s. Mid to low tide for best shape; high tide for softer waves. Offshore E/SE wind. Handles cross-shore wind better than Banana Beach.

Devil's Rock — Advanced

Devil's Rock is Tamraght's most challenging and rewarding surf spot. This exposed reef break produces powerful, hollow lefts and rights that barrel on their best days. The take-off is steep and critical — there is no time to hesitate. The reef is shallow, especially at low tide, and the currents can be strong.

This is a wave for experienced surfers only. If you are not comfortable in overhead waves, do not paddle out here. If you are, Devil's Rock offers some of the best barrels on this stretch of coast — and with far fewer crowds than the Taghazout points. A local guide is essential for your first session. They will tell you the exact take-off spot, the tide window, and how to navigate the reef entry and exit.

Best conditions: NW swell 310–330°, 4–10 ft at 12–16s. Mid-tide (rising preferred). Offshore E/SE wind essential. Best October–April. Do not attempt at low tide — the reef is dangerously shallow.

Warning — Experienced Surfers Only

Devil's Rock has injured surfers who underestimated it. The reef is sharp, the currents are strong, and the take-off zone is small. Always surf it with a buddy, ideally with a local guide who knows the spot. Wear reef booties. Check the tide. Check the swell direction. Do not paddle out on a big day without watching the lineup for at least 15 minutes from the cliff.

Spider Beach — Intermediate to Advanced

Spider Beach sits between Banana Beach and the Taghazout points, offering a reef break that is more forgiving than Devil's Rock but still demands respect. The wave breaks over a mix of rock and sand, producing fun, rippable rights and lefts that intermediate surfers can handle on smaller days and advanced surfers can push on bigger ones.

Spider Beach is often overlooked — while crowds pack into Anchor Point and surf schools fill Banana Beach, Spider sits in a happy middle ground. On a good day, you might share it with only a handful of surfers. It is a favourite of local guides who want a quality wave without the stress of a competitive lineup.

Best conditions: NW swell, 3–8 ft at 10–14s. Mid-tide. Offshore E/SE wind. Works best October–April but can produce fun waves year-round.

Pro Tip from Our Guides

Spider Beach is the guide's secret weapon. When Anchor Point is a zoo and Banana Beach is too small, Spider often delivers clean, uncrowded waves. Ask your guide if Spider is working — they will check it on the drive past and make the call.

How to Choose the Right Spot for Your Level

  • Absolute beginner (never surfed or <1 year): Banana Beach only. Book a camp with daily lessons. Let your instructor choose the best section of the beach for your ability.
  • Improving beginner (catching unbroken waves, riding down the line): Banana Beach main peak, or the mellower sections of Crocodile Beach on small days.
  • Intermediate (confident on green waves, working on turns): Crocodile Beach and Spider Beach. Ask your guide if conditions are suitable for a first point break session at Hash Point.
  • Advanced (comfortable overhead, reef experience): Devil's Rock and Spider Beach on their best days, plus the Taghazout point breaks (Anchor Point, Killer Point) which are a 10-minute drive away.

Seasonal Spot Guide

  • Winter (Dec–Feb): The reef breaks (Devil's Rock, Spider Beach) light up with NW groundswells. Banana Beach becomes powerful — beginners should stick to the inside reforms.
  • Spring (Mar–May): A golden window. All spots work well. Reef breaks are consistent but less intimidating. The weather is glorious.
  • Summer (Jun–Sep): Banana Beach and Crocodile Beach are the go-to spots. The reef breaks are generally too small to break properly.
  • Autumn (Oct–Nov): The season reignites. All spots come back to life. A fantastic time for intermediates to surf the reef breaks on manageable swells.

Spot-Hopping Checklist

  • Check the daily forecast — swell direction and size determine which spots work
  • Bring reef booties even if you plan to surf Banana Beach — you might end up at Spider
  • Trust your guide's spot selection — they know which break works on which conditions
  • Be honest about your level — a guide cannot keep you safe if you overstate your ability
  • Check the tide chart — reef breaks have specific tide windows
  • Bring water and snacks — some spots have no facilities
  • Watch the lineup for 5–10 minutes before paddling out at a new spot
  • If it looks too big or you feel uncomfortable, speak up — there is always another spot

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Tamraght surf spot is best for beginners?
Banana Beach (Plage Imourane) is by far the best spot for beginners in Tamraght. It is a long sandy beach break with consistent, mellow waves, a forgiving sand bottom, and plenty of space for surf schools. The waves are predictable and the entry is easy.
Is Devil's Rock dangerous?
Devil's Rock is an advanced-level reef break that can be dangerous for inexperienced surfers. The take-off is steep and critical, the bottom is rocky, and there are strong currents. It should only be surfed by confident surfers comfortable in overhead waves over reef.
How many surf spots are there in Tamraght?
There are four main named surf spots — Banana Beach, Crocodile Beach, Devil's Rock, and Spider Beach. Additionally, there are several unnamed peaks along the coastline that local guides know. Including Taghazout breaks just 10 minutes away, you have access to 10+ high-quality breaks.
What are the best conditions for Crocodile Beach?
Crocodile Beach works best on a NW to WNW swell (290–320°) of 2–6 ft at 10–14 seconds. Mid to low tide produces steeper, more hollow waves; high tide makes it softer. Light offshore wind from the E/SE is ideal. It handles wind better than the point breaks.
Do I need reef booties at Tamraght beaches?
Reef booties are not necessary at Banana Beach and Crocodile Beach (sand bottoms). They are strongly recommended for Devil's Rock and Spider Beach (reef bottoms). If you plan to surf the Taghazout point breaks, booties are essential year-round.
What tide is best for Banana Beach?
Banana Beach works on all tides, which is one reason it is so popular with surf schools. Low to mid tide produces slightly steeper, punchier waves for intermediates. Mid to high tide creates softer, more forgiving waves ideal for beginners.
Which Tamraght spot has the best barrels?
Devil's Rock is the premier barrel spot in the Tamraght area. On a solid NW swell, low to mid tide, and offshore wind, it produces hollow, powerful barrels. It is not a wave for the faint-hearted — the take-off is steep and the reef is shallow.
How do Tamraght spots compare to Taghazout spots?
Tamraght's spots are generally less crowded and more varied than Taghazout's. You have sand-bottomed beach breaks (Banana, Crocodile) alongside challenging reef breaks (Devil's Rock, Spider). Taghazout is dominated by world-class point breaks but lacks good beginner options.
Can I walk to the surf spots from Tamraght village?
Banana Beach is walkable from lower Tamraght (10–15 minutes downhill). Crocodile Beach is a 20-minute walk along the beach from Banana. Devil's Rock and Spider Beach require a drive (5–10 minutes). Most surf camps provide daily transport to all spots.
What is the best season for each Tamraght spot?
Banana Beach and Crocodile Beach work year-round, with the biggest waves in winter (Dec–Feb). Devil's Rock and Spider Beach need solid NW swells (Oct–Apr) to really turn on, with peak conditions in December–February. Summer is generally too small for the reef breaks.
Are there any secret spots near Tamraght?
Local guides know of peaks and reefs that are not on public surf maps. The coastline between Tamraght and Taghazout has several unnamed breaks that work on specific swell and tide combinations. The best way to access them is to surf with a local guide.
How crowded are Tamraght surf spots?
Significantly less crowded than Taghazout's famous point breaks. Banana Beach can get busy with surf schools in the morning during peak season, but there is plenty of space. Crocodile Beach, Devil's Rock, and Spider Beach are rarely crowded. Weekdays in shoulder season, you may have breaks to yourself.
