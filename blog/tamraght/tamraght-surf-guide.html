Tamraght — The Village

Perched on a hillside overlooking the Atlantic, Tamraght is a traditional Berber village that has quietly become one of Morocco's most beloved surf destinations. Unlike its bustling neighbour Taghazout, Tamraght has retained an authentic, slow-paced atmosphere. Narrow streets wind between white-and-blue houses. Men gather in cafes to drink mint tea and discuss the day's swell. Goats graze on the hillside. The call to prayer echoes five times a day.

The village is small — you can walk from one end to the other in 15 minutes — but it packs everything a surfer needs: surf camps, riads, local cafes, grocery shops, and a weekly souk. The hillside vantage point offers spectacular ocean views, and from almost any rooftop terrace you can watch the waves roll into Banana Beach below.

Why Choose Tamraght?

Tamraght appeals to a specific kind of surfer — one who wants great waves without the crowds, authentic culture without the tourist hustle, and value without sacrificing quality. Compared to Taghazout, Tamraght offers:

  • Quieter lineups. The breaks around Tamraght are less crowded than Taghazout's famous point breaks, especially in peak winter.
  • Lower prices. Surf camps, accommodation, and food cost 10–20% less than equivalent options in Taghazout.
  • Authentic atmosphere. Tamraght is still a real Berber village where families have lived for generations. Tourism exists alongside daily life rather than replacing it.
  • Access to everything. You are 10 minutes from Taghazout's world-class point breaks. The best camps drive guests to whichever spot is working best each day.
  • Varied surf. Within a 10-kilometre radius, you have beginner-friendly beach breaks, intermediate reef breaks, and challenging advanced spots.
Did You Know?

The name "Tamraght" comes from the Amazigh (Berber) word meaning "salt" — a reference to the salt flats that once existed in the area. The village's history predates surfing by centuries; salt trading and fishing were the original livelihoods here.

Surf Spots Around Tamraght

Banana Beach (Plage Imourane)

The crown jewel of Tamraght's beginner surf scene. A long, sandy beach break with consistent, mellow waves that peel gently to both the left and right. Ideal for surf lessons — the sandy bottom is forgiving, the waves are predictable, and the beach is wide enough to accommodate multiple surf schools without conflict. On bigger winter swells, Banana Beach can produce head-high waves that challenge intermediates. The beach itself is beautiful, backed by low cliffs with a handful of small beach cafes.

Crocodile Beach (Plage de l'Oued)

Named after the crocodile-shaped rock formation visible from the cliff, Crocodile Beach sits just north of Banana Beach. This is a more dynamic beach break with steeper, faster waves that suit intermediates looking to progress. The wave breaks closer to shore, producing hollow sections at low tide. At high tide, it becomes softer and more forgiving. A reliable option when Banana Beach is too small.

Devil's Rock

An exposed reef break for advanced surfers only. Devil's Rock produces powerful, hollow lefts and rights over a rocky bottom. On a good day with a NW swell and offshore wind, it barrels. The take-off is steep and critical — hesitation is punished. The rocky entry and strong currents make it unsuitable for anyone below a confident intermediate level. Best surfed on a mid-tide rising. A local guide is strongly recommended for your first session here.

Spider Beach

A reef break for intermediate to advanced surfers, Spider Beach offers fun, rippable waves that break over a mix of rock and sand. The wave is generally less intimidating than Devil's Rock but still demands respect. Works best on a NW swell at mid-tide. Less crowded than the Taghazout points, especially during the week.

Pro Tip from Our Guides

Tamraght's breaks work on a wider range of conditions than Taghazout's points. When Anchor Point is too big and chaotic, Banana Beach and Crocodile Beach often offer clean, head-high waves that are perfect for a fun, crowd-free session. The best local guides know when to skip the famous spots and head to Tamraght instead.

Best Season to Surf in Tamraght

Tamraght is blessed with surfable waves year-round:

  • October–April (Peak Season): Consistent 3–8 ft swells, offshore winds most mornings, water 16–19°C. December–February is biggest; March–April offers the best combination of waves, weather, and manageable crowds.
  • May–September (Summer): Smaller swells (1–4 ft) ideal for beginners and intermediates. Water warms to 20–23°C. The beaches are quieter, the prices are lower, and the vibe is relaxed. Dedicated surfers can still find head-high days every couple of weeks.

Where to Stay — Camps, Riads & Hostels

Tamraght has a high concentration of surf-oriented accommodation. Options range from shared dorms in surf hostels (â‚¬15–25/night) to private rooms in traditional riads (â‚¬25–50/night) to all-inclusive surf camp packages (â‚¬300–900/week). Most surf camps include accommodation, daily coaching, equipment, meals, and transport to breaks. The riads in Tamraght are particularly special — many are family-run, with rooftop terraces offering panoramic ocean views.

Local Culture & Berber Hospitality

Tamraght is an Amazigh (Berber) village, and the Berber culture of hospitality is central to daily life. Greet people with "Salam Alaykum". Accept mint tea when offered. Dress modestly when walking through the village — board shorts and a t-shirt are fine. Remove shoes before entering a riad. Friday is the holy day — the village is quieter than usual, and some shops close for midday prayers.

The local women's argan oil cooperative is worth visiting. You can watch the labour-intensive process of extracting argan oil by hand and buy directly from the women who produce it — a more ethical and authentic experience than buying from tourist shops.

Where to Eat & Drink

Tamraght's dining scene is smaller and more local than Taghazout's, but what it lacks in variety it makes up for in authenticity. Expect home-cooked tagines, fresh grilled sardines, couscous on Fridays, and the ubiquitous Moroccan mint tea. Most surf camps provide full board, so eating out is optional. A handful of local cafes and surf-themed eateries line the main road. For a wider range, Taghazout is a 10-minute drive.

"Tamraght is where you come when you want to feel Morocco, not just surf it. The rhythm of the village — the call to prayer, the sunset over the Atlantic, the silence of the hillside at night — that is what makes people extend their stay."

How to Get to Tamraght

Tamraght is about 40 minutes from Agadir Al-Massira Airport (AGA). Most surf camps include airport transfers. Grand taxis cost approximately 250–350 MAD (â‚¬25–35). The drive follows the coastal road north, passing through Aourir (Banana Village) before climbing the hill to Tamraght.

Non-Surf Activities

  • Hiking: The hills behind Tamraght offer excellent walking trails with panoramic views of the Atlantic and the Atlas Mountains in the distance.
  • Argan oil cooperative: Visit the women's cooperative to see how argan oil is made and buy directly from the producers.
  • Paradise Valley: A 30-minute drive inland takes you to this lush oasis of palm trees, natural pools, and waterfalls — perfect for a non-surf day.
  • Agadir souk: A 30-minute drive south brings you to one of Morocco's largest markets, with everything from spices to leather goods.
  • Camel rides: Several operators offer camel treks along the beach at sunset.

A Day in Tamraght

07:00

Wake up to the call to prayer. Breakfast on the rooftop terrace — msemen, eggs, fresh orange juice.

08:00

Your guide announces today's spot. Load the surf van. 5-minute drive to Banana Beach or Crocodile Beach.

08:30

Morning surf session — 2 to 3 hours with coaching. Warm up on the sand, then into the water.

11:30

Beach picnic. Fresh salad, bread, fruit. Mint tea from a thermos. Discussion of the morning's waves.

13:00

Return to the riad. Free time — nap, explore the village, visit the argan cooperative.

16:00

Afternoon free surf or theory session. Video analysis if your camp offers it.

19:00

Sunset on the rooftop. The call to prayer echoes across the valley. Dinner — tagine, couscous, grilled fish.

21:00

Mint tea, stories from the lineup, planning tomorrow's adventure. Tamraght sleeps early.

Tamraght Trip Checklist

  • Book your surf camp 1–2 months ahead (3 months for winter peak)
  • Fly to Agadir (AGA) — 40-minute transfer to Tamraght
  • Pack a 4/3mm wetsuit for winter, 3/2mm for shoulder, boardshorts for summer
  • Bring reef booties — essential for Devil's Rock and Spider Beach
  • Pack modest clothing for the village — cover-up, t-shirt, long trousers/skirt
  • Bring cash (EUR or MAD) — ATMs can run out on weekends
  • Download Windy + Surfline for daily surf forecasts
  • Learn "Salam Alaykum" and "Shukran" — Darija goes a long way
  • Visit the argan oil cooperative — support the local women's economy
  • Take a day trip to Paradise Valley or Agadir souk

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Tamraght good for beginner surfers?
Yes — Tamraght is one of the best locations in Morocco for beginner surfers. Banana Beach and Crocodile Beach offer consistent, mellow waves on sand bottoms, and there are dozens of surf camps with qualified instructors who specialise in teaching beginners. The village is quieter than Taghazout, which means less crowded lineups and more personal coaching attention.
How far is Tamraght from Taghazout?
Tamraght is approximately 5 kilometres south of Taghazout — about a 10-minute drive. Many surf camps in Tamraght make daily trips to Taghazout breaks (Anchor Point, Killer Point, Hash Point) when conditions suit, so you get access to the full range of spots while enjoying a quieter village base.
What are the best surf spots in Tamraght?
The best surf spots around Tamraght are Banana Beach (beginner-friendly sand-bottomed beach break), Crocodile Beach (intermediate beach break with steeper waves), Devil's Rock (advanced reef break with powerful barrels), and Spider Beach (intermediate-to-advanced reef break). All are within a 10-minute drive.
What is the best season to surf in Tamraght?
Tamraght has excellent surf year-round. October to April brings consistent 3–8 ft swells and offshore winds, with December to February being the biggest. The shoulder seasons (March–April and October–November) offer the best combination of good waves, pleasant weather, and manageable crowds. Summer provides smaller waves ideal for beginners.
Is Tamraght cheaper than Taghazout?
Generally yes. Surf camps in Tamraght tend to be 10–20% cheaper than equivalent camps in Taghazout for similar quality. Accommodation, food, and local services also cost slightly less. You get more value for money while being only 10 minutes from Taghazout's premium surf breaks.
What is the vibe in Tamraght compared to Taghazout?
Tamraght has a much quieter, more authentic vibe than Taghazout. It is a traditional Berber village where families have lived for generations. The streets are quieter, the cafes are local, and life moves at a slower pace. It appeals to surfers who want great waves without the party scene and commercial bustle of Taghazout.
How do I get to Tamraght from Agadir airport?
Tamraght is about 40 minutes by car from Agadir Al-Massira Airport (AGA). Most surf camps include airport transfers in their package. If arranging your own transport, a grand taxi costs approximately 250–350 MAD (25–35 EUR). The drive follows the coastal road north, passing through Aourir before reaching Tamraght.
Are there restaurants and shops in Tamraght?
Yes, though fewer than Taghazout. Tamraght has several local cafes and small restaurants serving Moroccan cuisine, a few surf-themed eateries, grocery shops, and a weekly souk. For a wider range of dining options, Taghazout is just 10 minutes away. Most surf camps provide full board, so eating out is optional.
What non-surf activities are available in Tamraght?
Tamraght offers hiking in the surrounding hills, visits to local argan oil cooperatives, camel rides on the beach, day trips to Paradise Valley, and excursions to the Agadir souk. The village itself is perfect for quiet walks, photography, and experiencing authentic Berber hospitality.
Is Tamraght family-friendly?
Absolutely. Tamraght's quiet atmosphere, gentle beach breaks, and slower pace make it ideal for families. Several surf camps offer family packages with separate rooms, kid-friendly meals, and instructors who can teach children. The lack of nightlife and party scene is a plus for families seeking a peaceful holiday.
What wetsuit do I need for surfing in Tamraght?
A 4/3mm fullsuit for winter (November to March) when water temperatures drop to 16–18°C. A 3/2mm for shoulder seasons (April–May and October). Boardshorts and a 2mm top are enough for summer (June to September) when the water reaches 20–23°C. Reef booties are recommended for Devil's Rock and Spider Beach.
Is there internet access in Tamraght?
Yes. Most surf camps and riads have Wi-Fi, though speeds can vary. A Moroccan SIM card from Maroc Telecom (bought at the airport) provides reliable 4G coverage throughout Tamraght and at the surf beaches. This is recommended for checking surf forecasts and staying connected.
