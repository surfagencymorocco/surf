Where Is the Best Area to Stay in Taghazout?

Taghazout is a compact village. The village centre is compact and walkable. The centre (around the main square and the mosque) puts you 2–5 minutes from cafés, restaurants, surf shops, and the beach. The hillside offers quieter riads with stunning ocean views but involves a 10-minute uphill walk.

If you're here primarily to surf, stay close to the centre. You'll roll out of bed, grab a coffee at a streetside café, and be on the sand in under 5 minutes. The centre also has the highest concentration of surf camps, equipment rental shops, and restaurants — everything you need is within a short stroll.

If you want peace and panoramic sunsets, the hillside is worth the walk. The higher elevation means sweeping views of the Atlantic, Anchor Point, and — on clear days — the distant outline of the Anti-Atlas mountains. The trade-off is a 10–15 minute uphill walk back from the beach after a long surf session. For many visitors, that view from the rooftop at sunset makes every step worthwhile.

Did You Know?

Taghazout was a sleepy fishing village until the 1970s when European surfers discovered Anchor Point. Today it hosts thousands of surf visitors every winter, yet it has largely retained its authentic Moroccan character — the call to prayer still echoes through the village five times a day, and you can often see fishermen hauling their catch onto the beach in the morning.

Riads — The Authentic Moroccan Experience

What is a riad? A traditional Moroccan house built around a central courtyard, often with a rooftop terrace. In Taghazout, riads range from simple family-run guesthouses to boutique hotels with artisan decor, plunge pools, and panoramic terraces.

A typical riad costs approximately â‚¬40–80 per night for a double room (2026 rates), includes a generous Moroccan breakfast, and often has a rooftop with ocean views. The authentic atmosphere is the main draw — intricate zellige tilework, carved wooden doors, potted plants cascading from the courtyard, and the smell of fresh mint tea drifting from the kitchen.

Benefits: authentic atmosphere, home-cooked food, personal hospitality from local families, quiet and peaceful environment. Many riad owners have lived in Taghazout for generations and are a wealth of local knowledge — they can tell you which beach break works on which tide and where to find the best tagine in the village.

Drawbacks: fewer amenities than a hotel, some are uphill, and hot water pressure can be inconsistent in older buildings. Not all riads have ocean views — if this matters to you, confirm before booking.

Surf Camps — The All-Inclusive Option

For most surf travellers, a well-run surf camp often provides the best value and convenience. For typically â‚¬550–750 per week for an 8-day all-inclusive package (2026 rates), you get: accommodation, two surf sessions per day with coaching, all equipment (board + wetsuit), airport transfers, and breakfast and dinner daily.

Benefits: everything is handled for you — no researching spots, no renting boards, no figuring out transport. Coaching is included, so you'll progress faster than surfing solo. Daily transport takes you to the best breaks based on conditions, not just the closest one. The social atmosphere means you'll share meals, surf sessions, and sunsets with like-minded travellers from around the world.

Drawbacks: less flexibility in your daily schedule. The camp sets the rhythm — breakfast at 8, surf at 9, lunch, second session at 3, dinner at 7. If you prefer to set your own pace, a riad + independent surf guide might suit you better.

Hostels — Budget-Friendly Surf Stays

Hostels in Taghazout: approximately â‚¬15–25 per night for a dorm bed (2026 rates), approximately â‚¬30–50 for a private room (2026 rates). They offer common areas, shared kitchens, surfboard storage, and an unbeatable social vibe. Best for: solo travellers, backpackers, and surfers on a budget who want to meet other people.

The best hostels are on the main street or near the beach — look for places with rooftop terraces, solid WiFi, and recent reviews that mention cleanliness and hot water. Some hostels also organise informal surf guiding or have partnerships with local surf schools for discounted lessons.

What to watch for: not all hostels have 24-hour hot water, some dorms can be cramped in peak season, and street noise can be an issue if the hostel is above a café. Read reviews from the last 3 months to get an accurate picture.

Villas & Apartments — For Groups & Families

If you're travelling with a group of 4–8 people, renting a villa can be excellent value. Approximately â‚¬80–150 per night for a 3–4 bedroom villa (2026 rates) with a full kitchen, a rooftop terrace, and sometimes a private pool.

Benefits: your own space — nobody else's schedule, nobody else's noise. Cook your own meals with fresh fish from the morning market. Perfect for families with children or groups of friends who want to surf together and share the cost.

Airbnb and Booking.com have good options in and around Taghazout. Book at least 3 months ahead for peak season (December–February), as the best villas go fast. Look for properties with surfboard storage, parking if you're renting a car, and an outdoor shower for rinsing off wetsuits.

Accommodation by Neighbourhood

Taghazout can be divided into three main areas, each with a distinct character:

Village Centre: Convenient and lively. Surrounded by cafés, restaurants, surf shops, and the main beach. Best for first-time visitors, solo travellers, and anyone who wants to be in the heart of the action. Noise level can be moderate — the call to prayer and morning scooter traffic are part of the experience.

Hillside: Views and quiet. The higher you climb, the better the panorama. Riads here tend to be newer, more spacious, and more tranquil. Best for couples, remote workers, and surfers who want peace between sessions. The 10-minute uphill walk from the beach is the price you pay for those sunset views.

North End (near Anchor Point): Closest to the break, fewer amenities. A handful of guesthouses and small riads sit near the cliff path that leads down to Anchor Point. You'll be the first in the water at dawn, but you'll need to walk 15–20 minutes to reach the village centre for dinner or groceries.

Local Knowledge

If you want to surf Anchor Point at dawn, stay on the north side of the village. You'll be a 5-minute walk from the cliff path. Roll out of bed, grab your board, and you're in the water while the rest of the village is still asleep.

What to Look For When Booking

Not all accommodation in Taghazout is created equal. Here are the key things to check before you book:

  • WiFi reliability — Check recent reviews. Most places have WiFi, but speeds vary widely.
  • Hot water availability — Standard in riads, not guaranteed in budget hostels.
  • Breakfast included — Most riads and surf camps include it. Hostels rarely do.
  • Surfboard storage — Essential if you're bringing your own board. Confirm before booking.
  • Rooftop access — A rooftop terrace transforms your stay. Sunset sessions with mint tea are a Taghazout ritual.
  • Proximity to the beach — Map it. "5-minute walk" sometimes means "5-minute steep uphill walk."
  • Cancellation policy — Flexible policies matter. Winter swells are reliable, but flights get delayed.
  • Recent reviews — We recommend prioritising reviews from the last 3–6 months, as accommodation quality in Taghazout can change between seasons. Places change fast in a seasonal village.

How Far in Advance to Book

The booking window in Taghazout depends heavily on the season:

  • Peak season (Dec–Feb): Book 8–12 weeks ahead. The best riads and surf camps fill up very quickly for Christmas, New Year, and February half-term. If you have your eye on a specific riad with a famous rooftop, book early or prepare to compromise.
  • Shoulder season (Oct–Nov, Mar–Apr): Book 4–6 weeks ahead. There is more availability, but the top-rated places still fill up. March is a particularly popular month — empty lineups and perfect weather attract returning visitors who book months in advance.
  • Summer (May–Sep): Book 1–3 weeks ahead. The village is quieter, and you can often find good last-minute deals. This is the best window for spontaneous trips and flexible itineraries.

Common Mistakes When Booking in Taghazout

Avoid these five common pitfalls that can sour an otherwise perfect trip:

  1. Booking a place on the main road — The coastal road (N1) runs right through the village. Rooms facing the road get scooter noise, café chatter, and early-morning delivery trucks. Always check if your room faces the road or the ocean.
  2. Assuming all riads have ocean views — Many don't. Buildings in the centre are tightly packed. If a sea-view terrace is important to you, confirm it explicitly and check recent guest photos — not the professional marketing shots.
  3. Not checking if breakfast is included — In riads it usually is. In apartments and some guesthouses, it is not. The difference can add â‚¬5–10 per person per day to your budget.
  4. Booking too far from the centre without a car — A hillside riad with a view sounds perfect until you're walking 20 minutes uphill in a still-damp wetsuit after a 3-hour session. If you don't have a car, stay within 10 minutes' walk of the centre.
  5. Ignoring cancellation policies — Winter swells are reliable, but travel disruptions happen. Flight cancellations, airline strikes, and visa delays are real. Book with a flexible cancellation policy or make sure your travel insurance covers accommodation.
Common Mistake

Be careful with Airbnb listings that show 'beachfront' locations. Some are actually across the main road or behind other buildings. Always check recent reviews and the map carefully. If the price seems too good to be true for a sea-view riad in December, it probably is.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best area to stay in Taghazout?
The centre village is the best area — walking distance to cafés, restaurants, surf shops, and the beach. Hillside riads offer panoramic ocean views but involve a 10-minute uphill walk. North end near Anchor Point is closest to the break but has fewer amenities.
How much does a riad cost in Taghazout?
A riad in Taghazout typically costs approximately â‚¬40–80 per night for a double room (2026 rates). Budget riads start around â‚¬25–30, while premium boutique riads can reach â‚¬100–120 per night. Most riad prices include breakfast.
Is Taghazout expensive?
No, Taghazout is very affordable compared to European surf destinations. A hostel dorm bed costs â‚¬15–25, a budget riad â‚¬25–40, and a mid-range riad â‚¬40–80 per night. Meals cost typically â‚¬5–10 per day (2026 estimates), and surfboard rental is approximately â‚¬10–15 per day (2026 estimates).
Are surf camps worth it compared to booking separately?
Yes, surf camps offer the best value. For typically â‚¬550–750 per week for an 8-day all-inclusive package (2026 rates), you get accommodation, 2 surf sessions/day with coaching, all equipment, airport transfers, and meals. Booking separately typically costs more and requires more logistics.
Can I find accommodation on arrival?
In summer (May–Sep), yes — you can often find last-minute rooms. In peak season (Dec–Feb), this is risky. The best riads and surf camps fill up 8–12 weeks ahead. Always book in advance for winter.
Are there hotels in Taghazout?
There are very few traditional hotels in Taghazout village itself. Accommodation is primarily riads, guesthouses, surf camps, and hostels. Large resort hotels are found 5–10 km away near Agadir. Taghazout's charm is in its authentic, small-scale lodging.
Is WiFi reliable in Taghazout?
Yes, most riads and surf camps in Taghazout have reliable WiFi. Hostels also typically offer WiFi. Speeds are generally good enough for video calls and streaming in most accommodations, but always check recent reviews to confirm.
Is Taghazout safe at night?
Yes, Taghazout is generally very safe at night — the village is small and community-oriented. The village has a relaxed, friendly atmosphere. Walking alone through the centre is fine. As with any destination, use common sense, keep valuables secure, and stick to well-lit streets after dark.
Do accommodations provide surfboard storage?
Most surf camps and hostels provide dedicated surfboard storage rooms with racks. Riad guesthouses usually have a secure area for boards — either a ground-floor storage room or a locked terrace. Always confirm board storage availability before booking if you are bringing your own board.
What's the best accommodation for solo travellers?
Hostels and surf camps are the best for solo travellers. Hostels (â‚¬15–25/night) offer a social atmosphere and shared kitchens. Surf camps provide an instant community with daily surf sessions and shared meals. Both are excellent for meeting other surfers and travellers.
Can families find suitable accommodation?
Yes. Villas and apartments are the best option for families — they offer multiple bedrooms, a kitchen, and private space. Many riads also have family rooms or can arrange connecting rooms. Book 3+ months ahead for peak season (Dec–Feb and school holidays).
Do I need to speak French or Arabic to book accommodation?
In our experience, most surf-oriented accommodations in Taghazout have English-speaking staff. Smaller, traditional riads may require some French or Arabic. Basic French phrases are appreciated but not necessary. Many riads and all surf camps can communicate fully in English via email or WhatsApp.
