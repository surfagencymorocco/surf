    <h2 id="how-we-evaluated">How We Evaluated These Camps</h2>
    <p>We assessed every camp against seven criteria that actually matter once you are here, in the water, at 7 AM with sand between your toes and a wetsuit that may or may not fit:</p>

    <ul>
      <li><strong>Price transparency.</strong> If a camp advertises â‚¬350 for 8 days and then charges extra for wetsuit rental, lunch, and transport, that is not a â‚¬350 camp. We looked at the real all-in cost after extras.</li>
      <li><strong>Coaching quality.</strong> Are instructors certified by the Royal Moroccan Surfing Federation (FRMS) or an equivalent body? Do they speak enough English, French, or German to actually teach technique â€” or do they just push you into waves?</li>
      <li><strong>Group size.</strong> A group of 4 students per instructor versus 10 students per instructor is the difference between a surf coaching holiday and a surf-themed beach trip.</li>
      <li><strong>Accommodation.</strong> Cleanliness, distance from the surf, noise levels, hot water reliability. We have stayed in enough riads with cold showers to know this matters.</li>
      <li><strong>Location flexibility.</strong> Does the camp drive 45 minutes to the best swell direction, or do they take you to the nearest break regardless of conditions?</li>
      <li><strong>Food.</strong> Moroccan cuisine is one of the best things about a surf trip here. Camps that serve reheated frozen tagine three days in a row are cutting corners.</li>
      <li><strong>Extras.</strong> Video analysis, surf theory sessions, yoga, day trips to Paradise Valley or the souk â€” these separate a good camp from a great one.</li>
    </ul>

    <h2 id="surf-ethics">Surf Etiquette in Moroccan Camps</h2>
    <p>This section does not appear in most surf camp guides, but it should. When you book a surf camp, you are joining a group that will arrive at a lineup together.     <p>A responsible camp teaches surf etiquette on Day 1 and enforces it in the water: do not drop in, do not paddle around someone sitting deeper, share waves on long point breaks, respect the local hierarchy.     <p>We also recommend asking your camp how they manage impact on local lineups. You do not want to be in that second group â€” for your own surfing experience and for the goodwill of the local surf community.</p>
